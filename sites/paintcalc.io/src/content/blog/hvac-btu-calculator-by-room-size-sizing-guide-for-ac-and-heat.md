# HVAC BTU Calculator by Room Size: Sizing Guide for AC and Heat

An undersized HVAC system runs constantly and fails to cool or heat. An oversized system short-cycles, wastes energy, and creates humidity problems. This guide shows how to calculate the right BTU rating for any room.

## What Is a BTU?

BTU stands for British Thermal Unit. One BTU is the energy needed to raise one pound of water by one degree Fahrenheit. In HVAC, BTU/hr measures cooling or heating capacity.

## Basic BTU Formula

For cooling: **Room volume (cu ft) x climate factor**

Climate factors:
- Hot/tropical: 20-25 BTU per cu ft
- Moderate: 25-30 BTU per cu ft
- Cold/northern: 30-35 BTU per cu ft

**Example:** 20x15 room with 9-foot ceilings in a moderate climate.
- Volume: 20 x 15 x 9 = 2,700 cu ft
- BTU needed: 2,700 x 25 = **67,500 BTU**

Wait, that seems high. That is because this rough method is for whole-house sizing. For single rooms, use the standard rule:

**Square footage x 20-25 BTU per sq ft**

Same room: 300 sq ft x 25 = **7,500 BTU** for a window unit or mini-split.

## Adjustments

- **Sun exposure:** Add 10% for heavy sun, subtract 10% for heavy shade.
- **Occupants:** Add 600 BTU per person beyond two.
- **Kitchen:** Add 4,000 BTU for stove heat.
- **Ceiling height over 8 ft:** Add 10% per extra foot.
- **Poor insulation:** Add 20-30%.

## Furnace BTU Sizing

For heating in cold climates: **Square footage x 30-60 BTU per sq ft**

- Well-insulated modern home: 30-40 BTU/sq ft
- Average home: 40-50 BTU/sq ft
- Poorly insulated older home: 50-60 BTU/sq ft

## Whole-House Sizing Example

1,500 sq ft home in cold climate, average insulation:
- 1,500 x 45 = **67,500 BTU furnace**

## The Professional Approach

HVAC contractors perform Manual J load calculations. These account for:
- Window size and direction
- Insulation R-values
- Air leakage rates
- Duct efficiency
- Local climate data

Manual J is more accurate than rules of thumb. Use our calculator for estimates, but hire a pro for permanent installations.

## Common Mistakes

- **Oversizing:** Causes short cycling, humidity issues, and premature wear.
- **Ignoring climate:** A Florida home needs more cooling capacity per square foot than a Minnesota home.
- **Forgetting occupancy:** A home office with three people and two computers needs more cooling.
