# How to Double a Recipe: Calculator, Tips, and Common Mistakes

Doubling a recipe seems straightforward. Multiply everything by two and you are done. Except in baking, where doubling can produce flat cakes, dense muffins, and overspilling pans. This guide shows how to double recipes correctly.

## The Basic Rule

For most savory cooking, doubling works linearly. Two times the ingredients, two times the yield. For baking, proceed with caution.

## Baking Adjustments When Doubling

**Leavening agents:** Baking powder and soda do not always need a full double. For doubled recipes, use 1.75x the leavening to prevent collapse.

**Salt and spices:** Double them, but taste test. Sometimes 1.5x is enough.

**Liquids:** Double them, but watch consistency. Some batters become too thin when doubled.

**Eggs:** Always double exactly. Eggs provide structure and cannot be rounded casually.

## Pan and Oven Considerations

Doubling a cake recipe usually requires two pans, not one larger pan. A doubled batch in one deep pan will:
- Take much longer to bake
- Risk burning edges while the center remains raw
- Overflow during rising

Use two identical pans and rotate them halfway through baking.

## Mixing Equipment

A doubled batch may exceed your mixer capacity. Stand mixers handle doubling better than hand mixers. For very large batches, divide into two bowls and mix separately.

## Cooking Time

Doubled volume does not mean doubled time. A doubled soup might only need 20% more time. A doubled cake in two pans needs the same time as the original. A doubled roast needs the same time if thickness does not change.

## Example: Doubling Chocolate Chip Cookies

Original: 24 cookies
- 2 cups flour
- 1 tsp baking soda
- 1/2 tsp salt
- 1 cup butter
- 3/4 cup sugar
- 2 eggs
- 2 cups chocolate chips

Doubled (with our calculator adjustments):
- 4 cups flour
- 1.75 tsp baking soda (not 2)
- 1 tsp salt
- 2 cups butter
- 1.5 cups sugar
- 4 eggs
- 4 cups chocolate chips

This prevents the spread and flattening that occurs with too much leavening.
