# Fuel Cost Calculator for Road Trips: Budget Your Gas Expenses

Road trips are great until you realize gas consumed half your budget. This guide shows how to calculate fuel costs accurately for any trip, including variables like terrain, driving speed, and vehicle load.

## The Basic Formula

**Fuel Cost = (Trip Distance / MPG) x Price Per Gallon**

**Example:** 500-mile trip in a car getting 28 MPG, gas at $3.50/gallon.
- Gallons needed: 500 / 28 = 17.86
- Cost: 17.86 x $3.50 = **$62.50**

## Round-Trip Calculation

Always calculate round trip unless you are flying back.

**Same trip round trip:** 1,000 miles
- Gallons: 1,000 / 28 = 35.71
- Cost: 35.71 x $3.50 = **$125**

## Variables That Affect MPG

The [U.S. Department of Energy](https://www.fueleconomy.gov/) documents how driving conditions substantially alter real-world fuel economy from EPA-rated figures:

| Factor | Impact |
|---|---|
| Highway driving | +10-20% vs city |
| Speed over 70 mph | -5-15% efficiency |
| Roof cargo box | -10-25% |
| Trailer towing | -30-50% |
| Mountain terrain | -15-25% |
| Cold weather | -10-20% |
| Aggressive acceleration | -15-30% |

## Multi-Stop Trips

For trips with multiple legs, calculate each segment separately if gas prices or terrain vary significantly. Gas is usually cheaper in some states than others.

## Budgeting Tips

- Add 15% to fuel estimates for safety
- Use apps like GasBuddy to find cheapest stations
- Drive 65-70 mph for optimal highway efficiency ([Car and Driver testing](https://www.caranddriver.com/) confirms 65 mph as the sweet spot for most vehicles)
- Remove roof racks when not in use
- Keep tires properly inflated

## Electric Vehicle Considerations

EV road trips require different planning. Use charging cost calculators and map charging stops. Home charging is cheapest, but DC fast charging on highways costs 2-4x more.
