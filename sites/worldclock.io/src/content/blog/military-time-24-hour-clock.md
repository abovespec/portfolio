Military time is the 24-hour clock system used by the US military, emergency services, hospitals, aviation, and much of the rest of the world. Instead of repeating 12-hour cycles with AM and PM, it runs from 0000 (midnight) to 2359 (one minute before the next midnight), creating an unambiguous way to express any time of day.

## How Military Time Works

Military time uses four digits. The first two represent hours (00–23) and the last two represent minutes (00–59).

- 0000 = Midnight (12:00 AM)
- 0600 = 6:00 AM
- 1200 = Noon (12:00 PM)
- 1800 = 6:00 PM
- 2359 = 11:59 PM

There are no colons in true military time notation — you write 1400, not 14:00. (The colon format is standard in civilian 24-hour notation, especially in Europe.)

## Military Time vs. 24-Hour Clock

The terms are often used interchangeably, but there's a subtle difference:

- **24-hour clock**: the civilian international standard (used in Europe, Asia, most of the world), written as 14:00 with a colon
- **Military time**: the US military's specific format, written as 1400 without a colon, spoken as "fourteen hundred hours"

The underlying system is identical. The difference is notation and pronunciation convention.

## How to Convert: 12-Hour to Military Time

### AM Hours (12:00 AM to 11:59 AM)

For times from 1:00 AM to 11:59 AM, drop the `:` and add a leading zero if needed. 12:00 AM (midnight) becomes 0000.

| 12-Hour | Military |
|---------|---------|
| 12:00 AM (midnight) | 0000 |
| 1:00 AM | 0100 |
| 2:30 AM | 0230 |
| 6:45 AM | 0645 |
| 11:59 AM | 1159 |

### PM Hours (12:00 PM to 11:59 PM)

For 1:00 PM through 11:59 PM, add 12 to the hour. 12:00 PM (noon) stays 1200.

| 12-Hour | Military |
|---------|---------|
| 12:00 PM (noon) | 1200 |
| 1:00 PM | 1300 |
| 3:30 PM | 1530 |
| 6:00 PM | 1800 |
| 9:15 PM | 2115 |
| 11:59 PM | 2359 |

**The formula:** For PM hours (except 12:00 PM), military time = standard hour + 1200. So 7:00 PM = 7 + 12 = 19 → 1900.

## How to Convert: Military to 12-Hour Time

For hours 0100–1159: subtract any leading zeros and add AM. 0830 = 8:30 AM.

For hours 1200: it's noon (12:00 PM).

For hours 1300–2359: subtract 1200 and add PM. 1800 = 1800 - 1200 = 6:00 PM. 2215 = 2215 - 1200 = 10:15 PM.

For 0000: that's midnight, 12:00 AM.

## How to Say Military Time

Military time is spoken differently from how it's written:

- 0600 = "zero six hundred hours" or "oh six hundred"
- 1200 = "twelve hundred hours"
- 1430 = "fourteen thirty" (not "fourteen hundred thirty")
- 1800 = "eighteen hundred hours"
- 2100 = "twenty-one hundred hours"
- 0045 = "zero zero forty-five" or "oh oh forty-five"

The word "hours" is optional in spoken context but common in formal military and medical settings.

## Why Use Military Time?

### No Ambiguity

The 12-hour clock creates potential confusion. "Meet at 9" — morning or evening? In a medical chart, 9 AM and 9 PM look nearly identical. Scheduling errors from AM/PM confusion have caused real-world problems in healthcare.

Military time eliminates this. 0900 and 2100 are unambiguously different.

### International Compatibility

Most of the world uses 24-hour time. When scheduling across countries, using 24-hour notation avoids the AM/PM convention that's primarily a US and UK habit.

### Software and Systems

Most computer systems store time internally in 24-hour format. Programmers, system administrators, and anyone working with log files encounters 24-hour timestamps constantly.

## Who Uses 24-Hour Time?

**Military time** (US convention):
- US Armed Forces (Army, Navy, Air Force, Marines, Coast Guard)
- Emergency services (EMS, fire departments) in many US regions
- US Navy navigators and officers

**24-hour clock** (civilian convention, used by default):
- Most of Europe (Germany, France, Spain, Netherlands, etc.)
- Most of Asia (China, Japan, South Korea, India, etc.)
- Latin America (most countries)
- Aviation and air traffic control worldwide (using UTC)
- Healthcare globally (hospital documentation)
- International telecommunications

**Countries that primarily use 12-hour time**: United States, Canada (informally), United Kingdom (informally for speech, though transport and official contexts use 24-hour), Australia, and a few others.
