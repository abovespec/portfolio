# eBay vs Amazon: Where Should You Sell in 2025?

Both platforms have millions of buyers. But they attract different shoppers, charge different fees, and reward different types of sellers. Here's a direct comparison to help you decide where to list.

## Fee Comparison

### Amazon Seller Fees

Amazon charges a **referral fee** (8–45% depending on category, typically 15%) on every sale plus either:

- **Individual plan**: $0.99 per item sold, no monthly fee
- **Professional plan**: $39.99/month, no per-item fee (worth it above 40 sales/month)

If you use FBA (Fulfillment by Amazon), add fulfillment fees based on size and weight, plus monthly storage. A standard item might cost $3–5 to fulfill. See our [Amazon FBA fee calculator](/) for exact numbers.

### eBay Seller Fees

eBay charges:

- **Insertion fee**: Free for the first 250 listings/month, then $0.35 each
- **Final value fee**: 13.25% of sale price (most categories), capped at $750 per item
- **Payment processing**: Included in the final value fee since eBay Managed Payments
- **Promoted listings (optional)**: 2–10% ad rate to boost visibility

For a $30 item: eBay takes roughly $3.98 (13.25%), vs Amazon's $4.50 referral fee + fulfillment. eBay is often cheaper on fees alone for self-shipped items.

## Buyer Audience

| | Amazon | eBay |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer intent | High — ready to buy | Mixed — browsing and deal-hunting |
| Price sensitivity | Moderate | High |
| Trust in sellers | Platform-first | Seller reputation matters |
| Best for | New goods, Prime shoppers | Used goods, collectibles, unique items |
| Returns expectations | Hassle-free, expected | More negotiable |

Amazon buyers search for products. eBay buyers search for deals. If you're selling a commodity product at market price, Amazon's intent-driven traffic converts better. If you're selling used gear or niche items, eBay's auction and best-offer model can get you above-market prices from motivated buyers.

## Product Type Fit

### Sell on Amazon when:

- Your product is new and sold by multiple sellers
- You can win or compete in the Buy Box
- You want hands-off fulfillment via FBA
- The category is high-volume (electronics, kitchen, health)
- You have a brand and want to register it (Amazon Brand Registry)

### Sell on eBay when:

- You're selling used, refurbished, or vintage items
- Your product is rare or hard to find elsewhere
- You want to test pricing with auctions
- You're liquidating inventory quickly
- You sell in collectibles, trading cards, or antiques

## Competition and Visibility

On Amazon, new sellers compete against established listings, private label brands, and Amazon itself in some categories. Getting visibility requires either winning the Buy Box or running Sponsored Product ads ($0.50–2.00 per click).

On eBay, every listing gets its own page. You compete on price, seller feedback, and listing quality rather than fighting for a shared product page. New sellers can get organic visibility faster.

## Selling Used Items: eBay Wins Clearly

Amazon allows used item listings in limited categories (books, electronics, some media). eBay has no such restrictions — you can sell a used coffee maker or a vintage leather jacket without category approval. If your business model involves sourcing and flipping used goods, eBay is built for you.

## Returns and Seller Protection

Amazon's return policy is buyer-friendly by default. Returns are largely automatic and often result in the seller absorbing the cost. Seller-initiated returns disputes are difficult to win.

eBay's "Money Back Guarantee" protects buyers, but sellers have more ability to contest unjustified claims and report buyer abuse. eBay's resolution center is more balanced in practice.

## The Verdict

| Goal | Recommended Platform |
|---|---|
| Sell a new branded product at scale | Amazon |
| Flip used items for profit | eBay |
| Lowest fees on low-priced items | eBay |
| Hands-off fulfillment | Amazon FBA |
| Unique, rare, or collectible items | eBay |
| Build a long-term private label brand | Amazon |

Many experienced sellers use both: Amazon for their core new-inventory catalog, eBay for liquidation, bundles, and hard-to-find SKUs. Start with whichever fits your current inventory type and expand from there.
