# 5 Day Workout Split for Intermediate Lifters: Full Program

Intermediate lifters have a specific problem: the beginner gains are slowing down, but ramping up volume arbitrarily does not fix it. A 5-day split solves this by distributing higher weekly volume across more sessions, hitting each muscle group with enough frequency to continue driving growth, and giving you the structure needed to manage fatigue intelligently.

This guide is for lifters who have at least 6–12 months of consistent training history, can no longer add weight to the bar weekly, and want a program that reflects their actual level.

For more on this topic, see [*How to Create Your Own Workout Plan: Step-by-Step Guide*](/blog/how-to-create-your-own-workout-plan).

## Who This Is For

You are an intermediate lifter if:
- You have been training consistently for 6–12+ months
- Your beginner linear progression (adding weight every session) has stalled
- You have solid form on the main lifts: squat, bench, deadlift, overhead press, row
- You can handle 15–20 sets per muscle group per week without excessive soreness

If you are still in the beginner phase, a 3 or 4 day program will produce better results with less stress on your schedule and recovery.

## The 5-Day Structure

This program uses a modified PPL structure with a dedicated arm day — a layout that works well for intermediates who have enough volume tolerance to benefit from specialization but still want full-body frequency.

| Day | Focus |
|-----|-------|
| Monday | Push (Chest + Shoulders + Triceps) |
| Tuesday | Pull (Back + Biceps) |
| Wednesday | Legs |
| Thursday | Upper (Strength Focus) |
| Friday | Arms + Weak Points |
| Saturday–Sunday | Rest |

For more on this topic, see [*Upper Lower Split Workout Program: 4-Day Plan for Strength and Size*](/blog/upper-lower-split-workout-program).

The Thursday upper session functions as a second frequency hit for the chest, back, and shoulders at lower volume and heavier loads — shifting the training stimulus to complement rather than duplicate the earlier push and pull days.

## Monday: Push

- Barbell bench press: 4 sets x 6-8 reps
- Overhead barbell press: 3 sets x 8-10 reps
- Incline dumbbell press: 3 sets x 10-12 reps
- Cable lateral raise: 4 sets x 15 reps
- Pec deck fly: 3 sets x 12-15 reps
- Tricep pushdown (cable): 3 sets x 12-15 reps
- Overhead tricep extension: 3 sets x 12 reps

## Tuesday: Pull

- Weighted pull-up or lat pulldown: 4 sets x 6-8 reps
- Barbell row: 4 sets x 6-8 reps
- Single-arm dumbbell row: 3 sets x 10-12 reps per side
- Cable row: 3 sets x 12 reps
- Face pull: 3 sets x 15-20 reps
- Barbell or dumbbell curl: 3 sets x 10-12 reps
- Hammer curl: 2 sets x 12 reps

## Wednesday: Legs

- Barbell back squat: 4 sets x 6-8 reps
- Romanian deadlift: 4 sets x 8-10 reps
- Leg press: 3 sets x 12-15 reps
- Bulgarian split squat: 3 sets x 10 reps per leg
- Leg curl machine: 3 sets x 12-15 reps
- Calf raise: 4 sets x 15-20 reps

## Thursday: Upper (Strength Emphasis)

- Barbell bench press: 3 sets x 4-5 reps (heavier than Monday)
- Weighted pull-up: 3 sets x 4-6 reps
- Overhead press: 3 sets x 5-6 reps
- Cable row: 3 sets x 8-10 reps
- Dips (weighted): 3 sets x 8-10 reps

Thursday is shorter and heavier than the early-week sessions. The goal is to hit the main movement patterns at higher intensity to build the strength base that supports hypertrophy work over time.

## Friday: Arms and Weak Points

- Incline dumbbell curl: 3 sets x 12 reps
- Concentration curl: 3 sets x 12 reps per arm
- Cable curl: 3 sets x 15 reps
- Close-grip bench press or bar dips: 3 sets x 10-12 reps
- Cable tricep pushdown: 3 sets x 15 reps
- Overhead dumbbell extension: 3 sets x 12 reps
- **Weak point work:** 2–3 sets targeting whatever you want to develop further

The weak point section is intentional. As an intermediate, you already know what lags — add lateral raises if your shoulders are narrow, add rear delt work if your posture suffers, or add direct ab work. This slot is yours to customize.

## Managing Fatigue at 5 Days Per Week

Five days per week is demanding. The main risk is accumulating fatigue that degrades performance on your main lifts. Manage it by:

**Programming deload weeks.** Every 4–6 weeks, reduce volume by 40–50% for one week. Keep the exercises, reduce the sets and load. This allows full recovery and typically leads to a performance jump the week after.

**Protecting sleep.** At 5 training days per week, sleep quality is not optional. Seven to nine hours per night supports hormonal recovery that directly drives muscle growth.

**Eating at maintenance or above.** Cutting calories while running this volume will compromise recovery and strength. If fat loss is the goal, a 4-day program with a modest deficit is more practical.

## Progressive Overload for Intermediates

Beginners add weight every session. Intermediates add weight every week or every two weeks. Some movements progress monthly. This is normal — not a sign the program is failing.

The most effective intermediate progression method is double progression:
1. Work within a rep range (e.g., 3x8-10)
2. When you hit the top of the range on all sets, add weight at the next session
3. Drop back to the bottom of the range and work back up

Log every session. Use our [workout planner](/) to track weights, reps, and sets so you always know your last performance and have a clear target to beat.

## Volume Targets Per Week

At the intermediate level, you should be hitting approximately:
- **Chest:** 14–18 sets
- **Back:** 16–20 sets
- **Shoulders:** 12–16 sets
- **Quads/Hamstrings:** 14–18 sets each
- **Biceps/Triceps:** 10–14 sets each

This program lands within those ranges. If you find a muscle group lagging, add one extra set per session for that muscle before making larger program changes.

## How Long to Run This Program

Six months minimum. Intermediate programs have longer adaptation windows than beginner programs. Do not evaluate this program at six weeks — evaluate it at six months. Keep a log, track your lifts, and look at progress over the full time horizon.

Most lifters who stick to a 5-day intermediate program for six months see meaningful increases in every major lift and visible physique changes. The structure works. The variable is execution.
