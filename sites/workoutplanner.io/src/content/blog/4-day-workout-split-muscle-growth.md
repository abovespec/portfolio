# 4 Day Workout Split for Muscle Growth: Hypertrophy Program

Building muscle requires hitting a specific target: enough volume to stimulate growth, enough rest to recover and adapt, and progressive overload over time to drive continued adaptation. A 4-day split delivers all three without overloading your schedule. This guide lays out a hypertrophy-optimized 4-day program designed specifically for muscle growth — not powerlifting, not fat loss, not general fitness. Muscle.

## Why 4 Days Is a Hypertrophy Sweet Spot

Three days per week is excellent for beginners building their base. Five or six days per week is viable for advanced lifters with high recovery capacity. Four days per week is ideal for intermediate lifters who want meaningful volume across all major muscle groups without accumulating excessive fatigue.

With four sessions, you can dedicate more sets per muscle group than a 3-day full body program allows, while still having three full rest days per week for recovery. Research supports 10–20 weekly sets per muscle group as the primary hypertrophy range for most people. A 4-day split makes that achievable without every session running 90 minutes.

## The Split Structure

This program uses an upper/lower structure with an A/B variation for each day. The emphasis across all four days is muscle hypertrophy: moderate to heavy loads in the 8–15 rep range, controlled tempos, and sufficient rest between sets.

**Recommended Schedule:**
- Monday: Upper A
- Tuesday: Lower A
- Wednesday: Rest
- Thursday: Upper B
- Friday: Lower B
- Saturday–Sunday: Rest

## Upper A — Chest and Back Focus

- Barbell bench press: 4 sets x 8-10 reps
- Barbell row: 4 sets x 8-10 reps
- Incline dumbbell press: 3 sets x 10-12 reps
- Cable row: 3 sets x 12 reps
- Pec deck fly: 3 sets x 12-15 reps
- Lat pulldown: 3 sets x 12 reps
- Dumbbell curl: 3 sets x 12-15 reps

Rest 2 minutes between compound sets. Rest 60–90 seconds between accessory sets.

## Lower A — Quad Focus

- Barbell back squat: 4 sets x 8-10 reps
- Romanian deadlift: 3 sets x 10-12 reps
- Leg press: 3 sets x 12-15 reps
- Leg extension: 3 sets x 15 reps
- Leg curl machine: 3 sets x 12 reps
- Calf raise: 4 sets x 15-20 reps

The squat is the centerpiece. Load it seriously. The rest of the session builds on that base.

## Upper B — Shoulders and Arms Focus

- Overhead barbell or dumbbell press: 4 sets x 8-10 reps
- Weighted pull-up or lat pulldown: 4 sets x 8-10 reps
- Cable lateral raise: 4 sets x 15 reps
- Face pull: 3 sets x 15-20 reps
- Incline dumbbell curl: 3 sets x 12 reps
- Overhead tricep extension: 3 sets x 12 reps
- Cable tricep pushdown: 3 sets x 12-15 reps

Upper B gives shoulders and arms direct work they did not get as a primary focus on Upper A. The pull-up or lat pulldown here prioritizes back width (lats) over thickness, balancing the row-heavy Upper A.

## Lower B — Posterior Chain and Glute Focus

- Romanian deadlift: 4 sets x 10-12 reps
- Bulgarian split squat: 3 sets x 10-12 reps per leg
- Leg press: 3 sets x 15 reps
- Leg curl machine: 4 sets x 12-15 reps
- Hip thrust (barbell or machine): 3 sets x 12 reps
- Calf raise: 3 sets x 20 reps

Lower B shifts emphasis from quad-dominant (squat) to hip-dominant (RDL and hip thrust). This balance across the week ensures full posterior chain development and reduces the risk of knee issues from quad-dominant training.

## Hypertrophy Principles Built Into This Program

**Rep ranges.** The 8–15 range is well-supported for hypertrophy. Going heavier (4–6 reps) builds strength but not maximum muscle. Going lighter (20+ reps) works but requires high proximity to failure to be effective. This program stays in the productive middle range.

**Volume per muscle.** Count your sets: chest gets approximately 10 direct sets per week, back gets 12–14, legs get 13–15, shoulders get 7–8 plus indirect work. These numbers sit in the proven hypertrophy range.

**Mind-muscle connection.** On isolation work — curls, lateral raises, pec deck — focus on feeling the target muscle contract rather than moving the weight. This improves motor unit recruitment and muscle growth over time.

**Rest periods.** Short rest (under 60 seconds) limits how much weight you can use and reduces anabolic signaling. Rest 90 seconds on most accessory movements and 2–3 minutes on compound lifts. Do not rush.

## Progressive Overload Strategy

Track every session. Use our [workout planner](/) to log your weights and reps so you can see your exact previous performance.

Apply double progression: stay in the rep range until you hit the top of it on all sets, then add weight at the next session. For example, if your target is 3 sets x 10-12 reps on incline press and you hit 12, 11, 10, stay at that weight next session and try for 12, 12, 12. Once you hit 12 on all three sets cleanly, add 5 lbs.

## Nutrition for Muscle Growth

You cannot build muscle in a sustained calorie deficit. Eat at maintenance or a moderate surplus (200–300 calories above TDEE). Protein is the priority: 0.8–1 gram per pound of body weight daily. Carbohydrates fuel training intensity — do not cut them to very low levels if you want to train hard.

## How Long to Run This Program

Four to six months minimum. Hypertrophy programs reward consistency far more than variety. The most common mistake is switching programs after six weeks because progress feels slow. Muscle growth is a long-term process — visible changes take months, not weeks.

Run this split, add weight consistently, eat enough protein, and the muscle will come.
