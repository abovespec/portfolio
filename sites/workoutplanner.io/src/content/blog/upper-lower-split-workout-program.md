# Upper Lower Split Workout Program: 4-Day Plan for Strength and Size

The upper/lower split is one of the most effective training structures available, and it is frequently underrated because it sounds simple. Training four days per week, alternating between upper and lower body sessions, gives each muscle group high frequency, adequate volume, and enough recovery to grow consistently. This guide covers the structure, who it suits best, and a complete program you can start this week.

## How the Upper Lower Split Works

You train four days per week. Two days focus on the upper body — chest, back, shoulders, biceps, triceps. Two days focus on the lower body — quads, hamstrings, glutes, calves, and core.

The most common schedule is:
- Monday: Upper
- Tuesday: Lower
- Wednesday: Rest
- Thursday: Upper
- Friday: Lower
- Saturday–Sunday: Rest

This hits each muscle twice per week. Research consistently shows that training a muscle group two times weekly produces more hypertrophy than once-per-week training at the same total volume. You also get to practice the main lifts more often, which accelerates skill development.

## Who the Upper Lower Split Is For

This split works well across a wide range of experience levels:

For more on this topic, see [*Workout Split for Busy Professionals: Minimal Time, Maximum Results*](/blog/workout-split-for-busy-professionals-minimal-time-maximum-results).

**Late beginners** who have 3–6 months of full body training and want more volume without jumping to a 5 or 6 day program.

**Intermediates** who want reliable strength and size gains without the recovery demands of a 5-day split.

**People with busy schedules** who can reliably hit four days per week but not five or six.

It is not the best choice if you are training for a sport with specific physical demands or if you want to specialize intensely in a single body part.

## The Program

### Upper Day A (Strength Focus)

- Barbell bench press: 4 sets x 5-6 reps
- Barbell row: 4 sets x 5-6 reps
- Overhead barbell press: 3 sets x 8-10 reps
- Lat pulldown: 3 sets x 10-12 reps
- Dumbbell curl: 3 sets x 12 reps
- Tricep pushdown: 3 sets x 12 reps

### Lower Day A (Strength Focus)

- Barbell back squat: 4 sets x 5-6 reps
- Romanian deadlift: 3 sets x 8-10 reps
- Leg press: 3 sets x 10-12 reps
- Leg curl machine: 3 sets x 12 reps
- Calf raise: 4 sets x 15 reps

### Upper Day B (Hypertrophy Focus)

- Incline dumbbell press: 4 sets x 10-12 reps
- Cable row: 4 sets x 10-12 reps
- Dumbbell shoulder press: 3 sets x 12 reps
- Single-arm dumbbell row: 3 sets x 12 reps per side
- Cable lateral raise: 3 sets x 15 reps
- Face pull: 3 sets x 15-20 reps
- Hammer curl: 2 sets x 12 reps
- Overhead tricep extension: 2 sets x 12 reps

### Lower Day B (Hypertrophy Focus)

- Romanian deadlift: 4 sets x 10-12 reps
- Bulgarian split squat: 3 sets x 10-12 reps per leg
- Leg press: 4 sets x 12-15 reps
- Leg curl machine: 3 sets x 12-15 reps
- Walking lunges: 3 sets x 12 reps per leg
- Calf raise: 3 sets x 20 reps

## Why Two Different Upper and Two Different Lower Days

The A/B variation allows you to train with heavier loads at lower reps on one day and higher volume at moderate weights on the other. This combination of strength-focused and hypertrophy-focused work within the same split drives both adaptations simultaneously. It also reduces monotony across the week.

## Progressive Overload on This Program

On your A days (strength focus), aim to add weight to the main compound lifts every week. When you hit the top of the rep range cleanly, add 5 lbs on lower body movements and 2.5 lbs on upper body movements at the next session.

On your B days (hypertrophy focus), progress by adding reps first. Work through the full rep range, then add weight.

## Rest and Recovery

With four days of training, recovery is rarely a problem for most people. Focus on:

- **Sleep:** 7–9 hours. Growth hormone release peaks during deep sleep.
- **Protein:** 0.8–1 gram per pound of body weight per day.
- **Active rest:** Light walking on off days accelerates recovery without adding stress.

For more on this topic, see [*Progressive Overload Calculator: Track Weight Increases Over Time*](/blog/progressive-overload-calculator-track-weight-increases-over-time).

If you find the lower body days particularly draining, space them out. Training Monday, Tuesday, Thursday, Friday means back-to-back days. If recovery suffers, shift to Monday/Wednesday for upper and Tuesday/Friday for lower.

## Common Adjustments

**Too much volume on upper days?** Remove one accessory movement. The compound lifts are non-negotiable; the accessories are adjustable.

**Lower back fatigue from two RDL days?** Swap one RDL session for a leg press-focused session with a higher rep range. Romanian deadlifts are excellent but create more systemic fatigue than machine work.

**Want to add a fifth day?** Add a dedicated arm or weak point day. Keep it low intensity — 4–6 sets total — so it does not compromise the four main sessions.

## How Long to Run This Program

The upper/lower split is a long-term structure, not a short-term program. Run it for at least 3–6 months. When linear progression on the main lifts stalls, introduce periodization by cycling between 4-week strength blocks and 4-week hypertrophy blocks rather than switching programs entirely.

Most lifters who try upper/lower come back to it repeatedly because it delivers results without excessive recovery demands. Its balance of frequency, volume, and simplicity makes it one of the most consistently effective approaches in strength training.
