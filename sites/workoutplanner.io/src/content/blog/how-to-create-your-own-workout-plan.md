# How to Create Your Own Workout Plan: Step-by-Step Guide

Most people search for a workout plan online, follow it for a few weeks, and then search for another one. The cycle continues because the plan never quite fits — the wrong frequency, movements they cannot do, or goals it was not designed for. Learning to build your own plan breaks that cycle. You end up with something that matches your life, your gym, and your actual goals.

This guide walks through the process from scratch. No fluff — just the decisions you need to make and how to make them.

## Step 1: Define Your Primary Goal

Everything downstream depends on this. Be specific.

**Muscle growth (hypertrophy):** You want to be visibly bigger. This requires moderate to high volume (10–20 sets per muscle group per week) in the 8–15 rep range, a calorie surplus, and high protein intake.

**Strength:** You want to lift heavier weights. This requires lower reps (3–6), higher loads, longer rest periods, and heavy compound movements as the core of every session.

**Fat loss / body recomposition:** You want to lose fat and preserve or build muscle. This requires maintaining training intensity while eating in a moderate calorie deficit. Volume is slightly lower to account for reduced recovery capacity.

**General fitness:** You want to be healthier, more capable, and feel good. A balanced program with moderate strength and some conditioning work is the right structure.

Most people want a combination of muscle growth and fat loss. Pick the one that is most important right now and optimize for that. You can shift emphasis later.

## Step 2: Choose Your Training Frequency

Frequency is how many days per week you train. The best frequency is the one you can maintain consistently. A 3-day program followed perfectly beats a 6-day program you skip half the time.

Use this framework:

- **2 days/week:** Maintenance. Full body sessions. Fine for people with busy schedules who want to stay in shape.
- **3 days/week:** Ideal for beginners. Full body each session hits every muscle three times per week.
- **4 days/week:** Upper/lower split. Best balance of volume, frequency, and recovery for intermediates.
- **5 days/week:** PPL or modified PPL with a dedicated specialty day. For intermediates with time and solid recovery.
- **6 days/week:** PPL twice per week. High volume for experienced lifters with excellent recovery.

Choose the frequency you have confidently done before, or one step above your current level. Do not jump from 2 days to 6 days.

## Step 3: Pick a Split Structure

A split determines which muscles you train on which days. Your frequency drives this choice.

- **3 days:** Full body each session
- **4 days:** Upper/lower (Upper A, Lower A, Upper B, Lower B)
- **5 days:** Push, Pull, Legs, Upper, Arms — or any structure that fits
- **6 days:** Push, Pull, Legs repeated twice

For most people building muscle, the upper/lower split at 4 days is the most efficient structure available. It hits everything twice per week without daily training.

## Step 4: Select Your Exercises

Every session needs:

**1–2 compound movements** — exercises that involve multiple joints and large muscle groups. These are the foundation of any program.
- Squat, deadlift, Romanian deadlift (lower body)
- Bench press, overhead press, dips (upper body push)
- Pull-up, barbell row, cable row (upper body pull)

**2–4 accessory movements** — exercises that target specific muscles to add volume and address weak points.
- Lateral raises, face pulls, curls, tricep extensions, lunges, leg curls

Build sessions around one or two compounds, then fill in with accessories. Do not do 6 compound movements per session — you will not have the energy to do them justice.

**Exercise selection by muscle group:**

*Chest:* Barbell bench press, dumbbell bench press, incline press, pec deck fly, cable crossover

*Back:* Barbell row, pull-up, lat pulldown, cable row, single-arm dumbbell row

*Shoulders:* Overhead press, lateral raise, face pull, rear delt fly

*Quads:* Squat, leg press, lunges, Bulgarian split squat, leg extension

*Hamstrings/Glutes:* Romanian deadlift, leg curl, hip thrust, good morning

*Biceps:* Barbell curl, dumbbell curl, hammer curl, incline curl

*Triceps:* Close-grip bench, dips, pushdown, overhead extension

Pick exercises you can do with good form in your gym with the equipment available. An exercise you execute well beats a "better" exercise you perform poorly.

## Step 5: Assign Sets and Reps

Match your rep scheme to your goal:

| Goal | Reps Per Set | Sets Per Exercise | Rest |
|------|-------------|-------------------|------|
| Strength | 3–6 | 3–5 | 3–5 min |
| Hypertrophy | 8–15 | 3–4 | 90–120 sec |
| Muscular endurance | 15–25 | 2–3 | 45–60 sec |

For muscle growth, aim for 10–20 working sets per muscle group per week. Start at the lower end (10–12 sets) and add volume gradually over months as your recovery improves.

A typical session for hypertrophy looks like:
- Compound lift 1: 4 sets x 8-10 reps
- Compound lift 2: 3 sets x 10 reps
- Accessory 1: 3 sets x 12 reps
- Accessory 2: 3 sets x 12-15 reps
- Accessory 3: 2-3 sets x 15 reps

That is 15–16 working sets in one session — appropriate for a 4-day upper/lower split.

## Step 6: Build In Progressive Overload

Progressive overload is the mechanism behind all strength and muscle gains. You must make your workouts progressively harder over time. Without this, your body has no reason to adapt.

The simplest methods:

**Add weight.** When you hit the top of your rep range on all sets, add the smallest available increment at the next session.

**Add reps.** Use double progression: work within a rep range (e.g., 3x8-12). When you hit 12 on all three sets, add weight and drop back to 8.

**Reduce rest time.** Doing the same work in less time increases relative intensity.

**Add sets.** If you have been doing 3 sets of an exercise for months, adding a fourth set increases volume and drives new adaptation.

Use our [workout planner](/) to log every session. Without records, you are guessing whether you are progressing. With records, you have a clear target every session: beat the last number.

## Step 7: Schedule Rest and Recovery

Rest days are not optional. Muscles grow during recovery, not during training. If you train 4 days per week, you have 3 rest days. Use them for light activity — walking, mobility work, or nothing at all.

Consider a deload week every 4–6 weeks if you train at high intensity. Reduce volume by 40–50% for one week, keep the movements, and let your body catch up. Most people see a strength jump the week after a deload.

## Putting It All Together

A complete 4-day hypertrophy plan built using this framework:

- **Goal:** Muscle growth
- **Frequency:** 4 days (Mon/Tue/Thu/Fri)
- **Split:** Upper/lower A/B
- **Exercises:** Compound-first, 2–4 accessories per session
- **Volume:** 12–16 sets per muscle per week
- **Progression:** Double progression on all exercises
- **Deload:** One lighter week every 5 weeks

That is a complete, effective program. It is not complicated. The hard part is executing it consistently for months without changing it prematurely.

The best workout plan is one you built around your goals, your schedule, and your equipment — and then actually followed.
