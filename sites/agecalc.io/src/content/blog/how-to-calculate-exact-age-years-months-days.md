Most people know their age to the nearest year. Getting the exact figure—years, months, and days—requires a method. This article walks through the manual calculation step by step, including the edge cases that catch most people out.

For one-off lookups the [age calculator](/) is faster. But understanding the manual method is useful when you want to check a calculator's output, work through a historical date by hand, or teach the logic to someone else.

For more on this topic, see [*Age Difference Calculator: How to Find the Gap Between Two People*](/blog/age-difference-calculator-guide).

## The Three-Step Method

### Step 1: Calculate Complete Years

Take today's date. Subtract the birth year from the current year for a provisional year count. Then check whether the birthday has already occurred this year.

- If today is on or after the birth month and day in the current year: your age in years is (current year − birth year)
- If today is before the birth month and day: subtract 1, because you haven't completed that birthday yet

For more on this topic, see [*How Many Weeks Old Am I? Calculating Your Age in Weeks*](/blog/how-many-weeks-old-am-i).

**Example:** Born June 8, 1990. Today is May 22, 2026.

- 2026 − 1990 = 36 (provisional)
- Has June 8 passed in 2026? No — today is May 22.
- **Complete years: 35**

### Step 2: Identify Your Most Recent Birthday

Your most recent birthday is the last occurrence of your birth month and day that fell on or before today.

Using the same example: the most recent birthday was June 8, 2025 (June 8, 2026 is in the future).

### Step 3: Count Months and Days Since Last Birthday

From June 8, 2025 to May 22, 2026:

**Count complete months:**
- June 8 → July 8: 1 month
- July 8 → August 8: 2 months
- August 8 → September 8: 3 months
- September 8 → October 8: 4 months
- October 8 → November 8: 5 months
- November 8 → December 8: 6 months
- December 8 → January 8: 7 months
- January 8 → February 8: 8 months
- February 8 → March 8: 9 months
- March 8 → April 8: 10 months
- April 8 → May 8: 11 months

That's 11 complete months. Now we're at May 8, 2026. Remaining days: May 8 to May 22 = **14 days**.

**Full result: 35 years, 11 months, 14 days**

## The February Edge Case

The trickiest part of this calculation is February, because it has 28 or 29 days while most months have 30 or 31.

**Problem scenario:** Born January 31. What is your age on March 31?

For more on this topic, see [*What Is Chronological Age? Definition & How It's Measured*](/blog/what-is-chronological-age).

- Last birthday: January 31 (current year, if it has passed)
- January 31 → February 28 (or 29): Is this one complete month? The end-of-month convention says yes. When one date is the last day of a month, the "one month later" date is the last day of the following month.
- February 28 → March 31: another complete month.
- **Total: 2 complete months, 0 days**

**Reversed scenario:** Born March 31. What is your age on May 31?

- March 31 → April 30 (end of April): 1 complete month
- April 30 → May 31: 1 complete month
- **Total: 2 complete months, 0 days**

The rule: when the day-of-month doesn't exist in the target month, use the last day of that month as the month-boundary point.

## The Day Count Within a Month

After counting complete months, the remaining days are the gap between the "last complete month boundary" and today.

**Example:** Born September 5, 1985. Today is May 22, 2026.

- 2026 − 1985 = 41 (provisional); September 5 has not passed in 2026, so subtract 1 → **40 years**
- Most recent birthday: September 5, 2025
- Months from September 5, 2025 to May 22, 2026:
  - Sep 5 → Oct 5: 1 month
  - Oct 5 → Nov 5: 2 months
  - Nov 5 → Dec 5: 3 months
  - Dec 5 → Jan 5: 4 months
  - Jan 5 → Feb 5: 5 months
  - Feb 5 → Mar 5: 6 months
  - Mar 5 → Apr 5: 7 months
  - Apr 5 → May 5: 8 months
  - May 5 to May 22: 17 days
- **Full result: 40 years, 8 months, 17 days**

## February 29 Birthdays

If you were born on February 29, your birthday only exists in leap years. The standard convention in most countries is to treat March 1 as your birthday in non-leap years.

**Example:** Born February 29, 2000. Today is May 22, 2026.

- 2026 is not a leap year, so birthday falls on March 1, 2026
- March 1, 2026 has passed (today is May 22, 2026)
- Complete years: 2026 − 2000 = 26; birthday has passed → **26 years**
- Most recent birthday: March 1, 2026
- March 1 → April 1: 1 month
- April 1 → May 1: 2 months
- May 1 → May 22: 21 days
- **Full result: 26 years, 2 months, 21 days**

The next leap year birthday (February 29) falls in 2028, at which point this person will be 28 years old on that date.

## Verification: Working Backwards

To check your calculation, reconstruct the date:

Start from the birth date, add the calculated years, months, and days, and verify you arrive at today.

**Check for the first example (born June 8, 1990; result: 35 years, 11 months, 14 days):**

- June 8, 1990 + 35 years = June 8, 2025
- June 8, 2025 + 11 months = May 8, 2026
- May 8, 2026 + 14 days = May 22, 2026 ✓

This confirms the result is correct.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Forgetting to check if the birthday has passed this year.** The most frequent error: subtracting birth year from current year without adjusting for whether the birthday has occurred yet. This produces an age one year too high for people whose birthday is later in the year than today.

**Using 30 days for every month.** February has 28 or 29 days; this affects day counts in the final partial month.

**Counting the end date as a full day.** If today is May 22, you have lived 22 complete days of May—not including May 23. Day counts are always "completed days," same as year and month counts.

**Miscounting the months.** A methodical month-by-month count (as shown above) is safer than guessing. Count each "same day, next month" step explicitly.
