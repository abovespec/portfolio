## Twitter / X: 280 characters

Twitter doubled its limit from 140 to 280 in 2017. The practical sweet spot for engagement is shorter: tweets under 100 characters tend to get more retweets. The 280 limit is a ceiling, not a target.

**What counts toward the 280:**
- Text, spaces, punctuation
- Usernames in replies (@username is excluded from the count for the first mention in a reply thread)
- Hashtags (fully counted)

**What does NOT count:**
- Media attachments (images, video, GIF)
- URLs — Twitter wraps all URLs to t.co links of exactly 23 characters, regardless of the original URL length

So a tweet with a 120-character URL only uses 23 of your 280 characters.

**X Premium (paid):** Long-form posts up to 25,000 characters. These display as articles rather than standard tweets.

## Instagram: 2,200 characters for captions

Instagram's caption limit is 2,200 characters, but the feed truncates after approximately 125 characters with a "...more" link. Mobile users see even less before truncation.

**Practical implications:**
- Put your key message and CTA in the first 125 characters
- Hashtags can go at the end — they're effective but don't need to be visible above the fold
- Line breaks work in captions but require workarounds in some post interfaces (draft in a notes app first)

**Instagram bio:** 150 characters. One URL allowed (link-in-bio tools help here). No clickable hashtags in bio on desktop, but they work on mobile.

**Instagram Stories:** Text overlays have no published character limit but become visually impractical past ~200 characters per screen.

## LinkedIn: 3,000 characters for personal posts

LinkedIn's 3,000-character limit is generous, and longer posts perform well on the platform — LinkedIn's algorithm appears to reward content that keeps users reading. Posts between 1,500–2,000 characters (roughly 250–350 words) tend to see strong organic reach.

**LinkedIn truncation:** Posts show the first ~210 characters before a "...see more" link on desktop, and fewer on mobile.

**LinkedIn articles:** Separate from posts, LinkedIn articles allow up to 125,000 characters — effectively unlimited for content purposes. Articles are indexed by Google, making them useful for SEO.

**Profile sections:**

| Section | Limit |
|---------|-------|
| Headline | 220 characters |
| About / Summary | 2,600 characters |
| Experience description | 2,000 characters |
| Recommendation | 3,000 characters |

## TikTok: 2,200 characters

TikTok's caption limit matches Instagram at 2,200 characters, but most TikTok captions are far shorter — the content format doesn't reward long text. The visible preview before truncation is roughly 50–80 characters.

**Hashtags on TikTok** count toward the 2,200-character limit, unlike some other platforms. Adding 10 hashtags at ~20 characters each consumes 200 characters of your budget.

**TikTok bio:** 80 characters. The shortest bio limit of any major platform — every word counts.

## Facebook: Practically unlimited for organic posts

Facebook's 63,206-character limit for posts is effectively unlimited for content purposes. For **Facebook Ads**, the constraints are much tighter:

| Ad element | Recommended | Hard limit |
|-----------|------------|-----------|
| Primary text | 125 characters | 5 lines before truncation |
| Headline | 27 characters | 40 characters |
| Description | 27 characters | 30 characters |
| Link description | 30 characters | — |

Facebook ad copy that exceeds these lengths gets truncated — which is why short, punchy ad copy consistently outperforms long copy on the platform.

## YouTube: 5,000 for descriptions, 100 for titles

YouTube titles can be up to 100 characters, but search results display roughly 60–70 characters before truncation. Front-load keywords in your title.

YouTube descriptions allow 5,000 characters. Only the first 157 characters appear in search snippets; the first 2–3 lines are visible on the video page before a "Show more" button. Put your most important links and CTAs in the first 150 characters.

## Writing efficiently within character limits

**Use a live counter.** Pasting into a [character counter](/) while drafting shows you exactly where you stand without copy-pasting into platform boxes repeatedly.

**Front-load the message.** Every platform truncates at some point. Assume most readers won't expand the full text and write so the visible portion delivers the core value.

**Count hashtags separately.** On most platforms, hashtags count toward your character limit. Keep a running tally when planning hashtag-heavy posts.

**Tighten ruthlessly.** Common cuts: filler phrases ("In this post I'm going to..."), redundant adjectives, and "that" clauses. Every character saved is a character you can use for content.

## Platform-specific tips

**Twitter/X:** Use the URL compression (t.co = 23 chars) to your advantage — long URLs don't penalize your count.

**Instagram:** Write captions in Apple Notes or Google Docs where you can see the character count before copying to the app.

**LinkedIn:** The "see more" break point is ~210 characters. End the visible preview on a hook or question — give people a reason to expand.

**TikTok:** Keep bios under 80 characters and make them immediately clear about who you are. No second chances with such a tight limit.
