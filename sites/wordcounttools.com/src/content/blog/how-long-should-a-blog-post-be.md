There is no single correct blog post length for SEO. The right length depends on the keyword's search intent, competitive landscape, and content type. That said, there are clear patterns in what ranks — and what wastes your time.

## Word count by content type

| Content type | Typical word count | Notes |
|-------------|-------------------|-------|
| Quick definition / answer | 300–600 | Sufficient if the query has low competition |
| How-to guide (simple) | 800–1,200 | Covers the task without padding |
| How-to guide (complex) | 1,500–2,500 | Multi-step processes, multiple scenarios |
| Comparison article | 1,200–2,500 | Varies by number of options covered |
| Listicle | 1,000–2,000 | Depth per item matters more than total count |
| Opinion / commentary | 600–1,200 | Focused argument; length determined by content |
| News / announcement | 300–600 | Timeliness beats depth |
| Comprehensive guide / pillar | 2,500–5,000+ | Only justified by topic scope |
| Local landing page | 400–800 | Over-long local pages rarely rank better |

For more on this topic, see [*Word Count for a Resume: How Long Is Too Long?*](/blog/word-count-for-a-resume).

## What the data shows

Studies from SEMrush, Backlinko, and Ahrefs consistently find that **longer content ranks better on average** — but the relationship is complicated.

**The correlation:** Pages ranking in Google's top 10 average 1,400–2,000 words for most competitive queries. For highly competitive head keywords, top-ranking content often exceeds 3,000 words.

**The caveat:** Correlation is not causation. High-ranking long content tends to be comprehensive AND authoritative. Low-quality long content doesn't rank just by being long. Google has repeatedly stated that word count alone is not a ranking factor.

For more on this topic, see [*How to Check Word Count in Google Docs (Every Method)*](/blog/word-count-google-docs).

**The real driver:** Search intent. A query like "what is a spreadsheet" is fully answered in 400 words. A query like "best project management software for remote teams" requires comparing 8–12 tools with criteria, pricing, and use cases — which might take 3,000+ words to do properly. The length emerges from the topic, not from an SEO formula.

## Matching length to search intent

**Navigational queries** ("how to bold text in Google Docs"): Users want a quick answer. 300–600 words. A long post here doesn't help — it just makes them scroll to find what they need.

**Informational queries** ("what is compound interest"): A definition with context, examples, and related concepts. 700–1,200 words for most topics.

**Commercial investigation queries** ("best noise-canceling headphones under $200"): Users are comparing options before buying. 1,500–3,000 words to cover enough products meaningfully.

**Transactional queries** ("buy noise-canceling headphones"): These land on product pages or category pages, not blog posts. Length is less relevant.

## What competitors are writing

Before targeting a keyword, check the top 5–10 results. This tells you the content depth the algorithm currently rewards.

Use a [word count tool](/) to measure the top-ranking pages: paste the article text and check the count. If top results average 2,000 words, matching that depth is likely necessary (though not sufficient) to compete. If top results average 800 words, you don't need 3,000.

The goal is **topical completeness**, not word count. Ask: "Does this article fully answer what someone searching this keyword needs to know?" When the answer is yes, stop writing.

## When short posts rank

Short content can and does rank well when:

- **The query is specific and low-competition.** Long-tail keywords with clear answers are often won by the first page that answers them properly — at any length.
- **The site has strong domain authority.** An established site with topical authority can rank short posts on the merit of the broader site's trust signals.
- **Featured snippets are the target.** Google often pulls Featured Snippets from concise, direct answers. A tight 40–60-word definition in a well-structured post can win a Featured Snippet even if the full article is short.
- **The topic is inherently narrow.** Some queries simply don't have enough to say to fill 2,000 words without padding.

## The thin content penalty

Google penalizes thin content — not just by length, but by value. A 300-word article that fully answers a specific question is better than a 2,000-word article that repeats itself and pads with generic filler.

Signs of thin content:
- Repeating the same point in multiple sections with slightly different wording
- Introductions that describe what the article will cover rather than covering it
- Conclusions that summarize what was just said instead of adding new value
- "Also related" sections that pad word count without relevance

**Padding hurts.** Users bounce when they realize they're reading filler. High bounce rates and low dwell time signal to Google that the content didn't satisfy the query.

## Optimal structure for SEO content

Length matters less than structure. A well-organized article helps both users and crawlers:

- **One focused H1** that matches the primary keyword intent
- **H2s for each major subtopic** — these often map to related questions people search
- **Tables and lists** where comparison or enumeration is natural — these earn Featured Snippets
- **Short paragraphs** — 3–5 sentences for web; long blocks deter reading
- **A clear conclusion or summary** with a next step or CTA

Content that earns links, shares, and return visits will rank regardless of whether it hits an arbitrary word count target. Use a [word count tool](/) to measure what you're producing — then evaluate whether every word is earning its place.

## Summary: word count guidelines by scenario

| Scenario | Recommended length |
|---------|-------------------|
| Competitive head keyword | 2,000–4,000 words |
| Mid-tail keyword, moderate competition | 1,000–2,000 words |
| Long-tail keyword, low competition | 600–1,200 words |
| Local SEO page | 400–800 words |
| FAQ / definition post | 400–800 words |
| Pillar / hub page | 3,000–6,000+ words |
| News / trending topic | 300–600 words |

These are starting points, not rules. Always check what's currently ranking before committing to a length strategy for any individual keyword.
