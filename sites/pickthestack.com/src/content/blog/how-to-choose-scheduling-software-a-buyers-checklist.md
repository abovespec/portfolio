# How to Choose Scheduling Software: A Buyers Checklist

With dozens of scheduling tools on the market, choosing the right one feels overwhelming. This checklist breaks down the eight criteria that actually matter, so you can make a confident decision in under 30 minutes.

## 1. Number of Users

Solo practitioners need simple tools. Teams need round-robin, collective availability, and admin controls. Do not pay for team features if you work alone.

## 2. Calendar Integrations

At minimum, the tool must sync with your primary calendar. Check for Google Calendar, Outlook, and Apple Calendar support. Two-way sync prevents double-booking.

## 3. Payment Collection

If you charge for consultations or services, native payment integration saves hassle. Stripe and PayPal are the standard connections. Without this, you will chase invoices manually.

## 4. Custom Branding

Basic tools show their own logo. Paid tiers usually allow custom colors, logos, and domains. If client trust matters, branding is non-negotiable.

## 5. Reminders and Follow-ups

Automatic SMS and email reminders reduce no-shows by 30-50%. Check if the tool offers both, and whether reminder timing is customizable.

## 6. Team Scheduling Logic

For teams, you need round-robin (distribute evenly), collective (everyone must be free), and priority routing. Not all tools handle this well.

## 7. API and Integrations

Zapier support covers most no-code needs. If you have developers, a REST API opens deeper integrations with your CRM or database.

## 8. Compliance

Healthcare needs HIPAA. European businesses need GDPR. Finance may need SOC 2. Verify compliance before you commit.

## Try Before You Buy

Most tools offer free trials. Test the actual booking flow as if you were a client. If the experience feels clunky, your clients will feel the same.
