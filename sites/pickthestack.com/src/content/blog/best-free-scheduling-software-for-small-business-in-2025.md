# Best Free Scheduling Software for Small Business in 2025

Small businesses need scheduling software that is free, reliable, and easy for clients to use. The right tool eliminates the back-and-forth of finding a meeting time, reduces no-shows, and lets you look professional from day one — without spending anything.

This guide compares the best free scheduling tools available in 2025, with honest looks at their limitations and who each one is best suited for.

## What to Look for in Free Scheduling Software

Before picking a tool, clarify what "free" means to you:

- **Truly free vs. freemium**: Some tools offer a permanent free tier; others give you a trial. Know which you are evaluating.
- **Event type limits**: Many free tiers cap you at one event type (e.g., a 30-minute meeting), which is limiting if you offer multiple services.
- **Calendar integration**: Does it connect with Google Calendar, Outlook, or Apple Calendar? Conflicts are painful without real-time sync.
- **Branding**: Free tiers typically show the tool's branding on your booking page. That matters for first impressions.
- **Payment collection**: If you want to charge for appointments (consultations, sessions), most free tiers do not include payment processing.

## 1. Cal.com (Free Tier)

Cal.com is the most generous free scheduling tool in 2025. The free plan includes unlimited event types, one connected calendar, basic integrations with video conferencing tools like Zoom and Google Meet, and the option to self-host the entire platform on your own infrastructure.

The interface is clean and modern. Clients get a smooth booking experience, and you can customize availability, buffer times, and confirmation messages without paying.

The catch: some power features (team scheduling, round-robin routing, workflows) require a paid plan, and self-hosting requires technical comfort with deploying a Node.js app. For most small businesses using the hosted version, the free tier is fully functional.

**Best for**: Freelancers and small teams who want Calendly-level polish with more event types for free.

## 2. Calendly (Free Tier)

Calendly is the most recognized scheduling link in professional settings. Clients already know what a Calendly link means, which removes hesitation. The free plan includes one event type, one calendar connection, and unlimited meetings of that type.

The one-event-type restriction is the main friction point. If you offer a 30-minute intro call and a 60-minute consultation, you can only create one on the free tier, forcing manual workarounds or awkward naming conventions.

Calendly also shows its own branding prominently on free booking pages. This is a minor cosmetic issue for most, but noticeable in high-touch service businesses.

**Best for**: Solopreneurs who offer a single type of appointment and want client familiarity built in.

## 3. Microsoft Bookings

Microsoft Bookings is included with Microsoft 365 Business Basic ($6/user/month). If you already use Outlook and Teams, it integrates seamlessly — booking slots pull from your Outlook calendar, confirmations appear in Teams, and clients can browse your services like a simple booking page.

It is not truly free standalone, but if your business already pays for Microsoft 365, it is effectively free. The feature set is solid: multiple staff members, services with different durations and pricing, and a public booking page.

Setup is less intuitive than Cal.com or Calendly, but Microsoft's help documentation is thorough.

**Best for**: Small businesses already in the Microsoft 365 ecosystem who want booking without adding another tool.

## 4. When2meet

When2meet is completely free, requires no account creation, and solves one specific problem well: finding a time that works for a group of people. You create a poll with available time slots, share the link, and participants mark when they are free. The overlap grid makes the best time obvious.

It is not designed for client-facing booking — there is no calendar sync, no confirmation emails, and no way to take payment. But for internal team scheduling, contractor coordination, or organizing group calls, it is the fastest tool available.

**Best for**: Internal scheduling polls, not client booking.

## 5. Doodle (Free Tier)

Doodle serves a similar purpose to When2meet but with a more polished UI and account-based features. The free tier lets you create unlimited meeting polls, gather availability from participants, and see the best time at a glance.

The free plan shows ads, which can look unprofessional when sharing with clients. The paid tier removes ads and adds calendar integration, but the free version is perfectly usable for coordinating with teams or vendors.

**Best for**: Meeting coordination when you want a cleaner interface than When2meet.

## When to Upgrade from Free

The free tier is enough when you offer one type of meeting and do not need payment collection, custom branding, or team features. You should consider a paid plan when:

- You offer multiple service types with different durations or prices
- You want your booking page free of third-party branding
- You need to accept payment at booking (deposits, session fees)
- You manage a team and need round-robin or pooled availability
- You want automated reminder emails or follow-up sequences

Most small businesses will outgrow free scheduling software within a year of growth. Starting with Cal.com or Calendly free gives you time to evaluate what features actually matter before committing to a subscription.

## Recommendation

Start with **Cal.com** if you need multiple event types and do not mind a slightly newer interface. Stick with **Calendly** if you want the most recognizable booking link and only need one event type. Use **Microsoft Bookings** if you already live in Outlook and Teams. For group availability polling only, **When2meet** or **Doodle** are both excellent and require no commitment.
