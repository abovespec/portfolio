"What is 20% of 85?" This is one of the most common percentage questions, and the answer comes from a single, simple formula. Once you understand what the question is actually asking, the calculation becomes automatic.

## What "percentage of a number" means

When you find a percentage of a number, you are extracting a portion of that number. "20% of 85" means "20 parts out of every 100 parts of 85." It is a way of scaling the number down to a fraction.

For more on this topic, see [*How to Calculate Percentage Increase: Formula and Step-by-Step Guide*](/blog/how-to-calculate-percentage-increase).

A percent is just another way of writing a fraction with denominator 100:

```
20% = 20/100 = 0.20
```

Finding 20% of 85 is identical to multiplying 85 by 0.20.

## The formula

```
Part = (Percentage / 100) × Whole
```

Or equivalently:

```
Part = Decimal Equivalent × Whole
```

The decimal equivalent of any percentage is that percentage divided by 100.

## Worked examples

### Example 1 — Restaurant tip

Your restaurant bill is $64. You want to leave a 18% tip. How much is that?

```
(18 / 100) × 64 = 0.18 × 64 = $11.52
```

Leave $11.52 as a tip.

### Example 2 — Sales tax

A laptop costs $899 before tax. The sales tax rate is 8.25%. How much tax will you pay?

```
(8.25 / 100) × 899 = 0.0825 × 899 = $74.17
```

The tax is $74.17, making the total $973.17.

### Example 3 — Test score

A student scores 76% on a test worth 150 marks. How many marks did they earn?

```
(76 / 100) × 150 = 0.76 × 150 = 114 marks
```

### Example 4 — Commission

A real estate agent earns a 2.5% commission on a $375,000 home sale. How much does the agent earn?

```
(2.5 / 100) × 375,000 = 0.025 × 375,000 = $9,375
```

### Example 5 — Discount

A $220 coat is 35% off. How much do you save, and what is the sale price?

```
Savings = (35 / 100) × 220 = 0.35 × 220 = $77
Sale price = $220 − $77 = $143
```

Or directly: `(65 / 100) × 220 = 0.65 × 220 = $143`.

## Mental math shortcuts

You do not always need a calculator. Common percentages have shortcuts that let you estimate in seconds.

**10% — move the decimal one place left**
- 10% of $74 = $7.40

**5% — find 10%, then halve it**
- 5% of $74 = $7.40 ÷ 2 = $3.70

**20% — find 10%, then double it**
- 20% of $74 = $7.40 × 2 = $14.80

**25% — divide by 4**
- 25% of $74 = $74 ÷ 4 = $18.50

**50% — divide by 2**
- 50% of $74 = $37

**1% — move the decimal two places left**
- 1% of $74 = $0.74

**Building combinations:** For 15%, find 10% and add half of it (which is 5%):
- 10% of $74 = $7.40
- 5% of $74 = $3.70
- 15% of $74 = $7.40 + $3.70 = $11.10

## The three percentage questions and how they connect

"Percentage of a number" is one of three types of percentage questions. All three use the same relationship between Part, Whole, and Percentage:

| Question type | Example | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| Find the Part | What is 30% of 80? | `(30/100) × 80 = 24` |
| Find the Percentage | 24 is what % of 80? | `(24/80) × 100 = 30%` |
| Find the Whole | 24 is 30% of what? | `24 / 0.30 = 80` |

For more on this topic, see [*What Percentage Is X of Y? The Simple Formula Explained*](/blog/what-percentage-is-x-of-y).

For more on this topic, see [*How to Calculate Percentage Decrease: Formula, Examples, and Use Cases*](/blog/how-to-calculate-percentage-decrease).

Once you know any two of the three values, you can always find the third.

## Everyday applications

**Tips and service charges:** Most tipping is mental math — 20% of $55 = $11. Knowing the 10% shortcut (move the decimal) makes this instant.

**Tax calculations:** Sales tax is always a percentage of the pre-tax price. Different states and countries have different rates, but the calculation is identical.

**Pay rises and bonuses:** If your $58,000 salary receives a 4% raise, your new salary is `$58,000 × 1.04 = $60,320`. Your raise in dollar terms is $2,320.

**Nutrition labels:** A label might say a serving contains 18g of fat. If the daily recommended amount is 65g, that's `(18/65) × 100 = 27.7%` of your daily fat allowance.

**Finance:** Interest on savings accounts, loan repayments, and investment returns are all calculated as a percentage of the principal.

## Avoid this mistake: multiplying by the percentage, not the decimal

A common error is multiplying the whole number directly by the percentage:

```
Wrong: 20 × 85 = 1,700  (this is meaningless)
Right: 0.20 × 85 = 17   (this is 20% of 85)
```

Always convert the percentage to a decimal first (divide by 100), then multiply.
