## Mortgage Calculator — Estimate Your Monthly Payment

Buying a home is likely the largest financial decision you'll ever make. Before you fall in love with a listing, know what the monthly payment actually costs. Our free mortgage calculator gives you an accurate estimate of your principal-and-interest payment in seconds — no account, no email, no delays.

### What This Calculator Computes

Enter your **loan amount**, **interest rate**, and **loan term**, and the calculator returns:

- **Monthly principal + interest payment** — the base payment you owe your lender every month
- **Total interest paid over the life of the loan** — the true cost of borrowing
- **Full amortization schedule** — a month-by-month breakdown showing how much of each payment goes to principal versus interest

Most buyers focus only on the monthly payment, but the amortization schedule reveals the full picture: in the early years, a majority of your payment covers interest, not principal. Understanding this helps you decide whether making extra payments makes sense for your situation.

### What the Calculator Does Not Include

A mortgage payment shown here is principal and interest only. Your actual out-of-pocket monthly cost also includes:

| Item | Typical range |
|------|---------------|
| Property taxes | 0.5% – 2.5% of home value / year |
| Homeowner's insurance | $800 – $2,000 / year |
| Private mortgage insurance (PMI) | 0.2% – 2% of loan / year (if down payment < 20%) |
| HOA fees | Varies widely ($0 – $1,000+/month) |

Add these to the calculator output to estimate your total monthly housing cost, often called PITI (principal, interest, taxes, insurance).

### Interpreting Your Results

**A general affordability guideline** used by most lenders is that your total monthly housing cost should not exceed **28% of your gross monthly income**. A second rule extends this: total debt payments (mortgage + car loans + student loans + minimums on credit cards) should stay below **36% of gross monthly income**. These are the conventional front-end and back-end debt-to-income (DTI) ratios used for qualifying most conventional loans.

That said, individual lenders set their own limits. FHA loans, for example, allow higher DTI ratios in some cases. VA loans have no set maximum DTI but lenders typically apply their own overlays.

### How Mortgage Interest Works

Mortgage interest is calculated daily on the outstanding balance. When you make a monthly payment, interest owed since the last payment is satisfied first; the remainder reduces your principal. As the balance shrinks, each subsequent payment allocates a larger fraction to principal — a process called **amortization**. By the final payment, almost all of it is principal.

This structure means that making even one extra principal payment per year can shave years off your loan and save tens of thousands of dollars in interest over a 30-year term.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**What is a good mortgage interest rate?**  
Rates fluctuate daily with the broader bond market. Historically, a 30-year fixed rate below the 10-year Treasury yield plus 2 percentage points is considered favorable. Check current rates from the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) mortgage rate tools or shop at least three lenders before locking.

**Should I choose a 15-year or 30-year mortgage?**  
A 15-year mortgage carries a lower interest rate but a higher monthly payment. You build equity faster and pay far less total interest. A 30-year mortgage offers a lower required payment, which preserves monthly cash flow — but you pay significantly more interest over the life of the loan. The best choice depends on your income stability and other financial goals.

**What is PMI and when can I remove it?**  
Private mortgage insurance protects the lender if you default. It is required on conventional loans when your down payment is less than 20%. Under the Homeowners Protection Act, lenders must cancel PMI automatically when your loan balance reaches 78% of the original purchase price.
