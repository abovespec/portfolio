## ROI Calculator — Measure What Any Investment Actually Returned

Before you invest a dollar — in stocks, real estate, equipment, marketing, or any other asset — you need a way to compare options on equal footing. Return on investment (ROI) is that common language. Our ROI calculator computes both simple and annualized ROI instantly, so you can compare a six-month flip to a five-year hold to a one-year stock position without mixing time horizons.

### What This Calculator Returns

Enter your **initial investment**, **final value** (or **net gain**), and **holding period**. The calculator returns:

- **Simple ROI (%)** — total percentage gain relative to the initial cost
- **Annualized ROI (%)** — the per-year equivalent return, adjusted for time
- **Net gain / loss ($)** — the dollar amount earned or lost
- **Total return multiple** — how many times you got your investment back (e.g., 2.3×)

### The ROI Formula

**Simple ROI = (Net Gain / Cost of Investment) × 100**

Where net gain = final value − initial investment.

Example: You invest $10,000. The investment is now worth $13,500. Net gain = $3,500. Simple ROI = ($3,500 / $10,000) × 100 = **35%**.

The limitation of simple ROI is that it ignores time. A 35% return over two years is not the same as a 35% return over ten years. That's where annualized ROI comes in.

### Annualized ROI (CAGR)

**Annualized ROI = [(Final Value / Initial Value)^(1/n) − 1] × 100**

Where n = number of years. This is equivalent to Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR).

Using the same example over 3 years: Annualized ROI = [(13,500 / 10,000)^(1/3) − 1] × 100 = **10.5% per year**.

Annualized ROI is the right metric when comparing investments with different time horizons, or when evaluating whether a return beat a benchmark like the S&P 500's long-run average (~10% annualized).

### What Counts as a "Good" ROI?

Context matters enormously:

| Asset Class | Typical Long-Run Return |
|-------------|------------------------|
| U.S. equities (S&P 500) | ~10% annualized (nominal), ~7% real |
| Real estate (residential) | 4%–8% annualized, varies heavily by market |
| High-yield savings account | 4%–5% (current rate environment) |
| Small business investment | Highly variable; 15%–30% targets are common |
| Marketing campaign | Positive ROI is the baseline; 200–500% is strong |

The question is not whether 15% is "good" in the abstract — it is whether 15% is good relative to your alternatives and risk tolerance.

### ROI vs. IRR

For multi-year investments with multiple cash flows (like rental income or business dividends), ROI and CAGR can oversimplify. **Internal Rate of Return (IRR)** accounts for the timing and size of individual cash flows, making it more accurate for complex investment structures. For single-entry, single-exit investments, ROI and CAGR are sufficient and easier to compute.

### Common Uses for This Calculator

- **Stock and fund performance** — compare what your portfolio actually returned versus the benchmark
- **Real estate** — plug in purchase price, renovation costs, and sale price to find your actual return
- **Business projects** — evaluate whether a capital expense is justified by its revenue impact
- **Marketing spend** — calculate whether a campaign's revenue lift justified its budget
- **Side businesses and freelancing** — measure hourly ROI on time invested versus revenue generated

### What ROI Does Not Capture

Simple ROI doesn't account for:
- **Risk** — a 12% return on a speculative penny stock is not equivalent to 12% on a Treasury bond
- **Taxes** — capital gains taxes reduce your after-tax return significantly
- **Inflation** — subtract the inflation rate from nominal ROI to get real return
- **Opportunity cost** — the return you didn't earn on the next best alternative

For investment decisions, ROI is a starting point — not the final word.
