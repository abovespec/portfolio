## Retirement Calculator — Find Your Number

Retirement planning comes down to one fundamental question: will you have enough? Our retirement calculator helps you answer it. Enter your current savings, expected contributions, years to retirement, and target income — and see whether your plan holds up. If it doesn't, the calculator tells you exactly what needs to change.

### What This Calculator Models

Enter your **current retirement savings**, **monthly contribution**, **expected annual return**, **years to retirement**, and **expected annual income need in retirement**. The calculator returns:

- **Projected balance at retirement**
- **Estimated years your savings will last** (based on withdrawal rate and return assumptions)
- **Monthly savings gap** — how much more you'd need to save to hit your income target
- **Year-by-year accumulation chart**

### The 4% Rule — A Starting Point

The most widely cited retirement withdrawal guideline is the **4% rule**, developed from the Trinity Study. It suggests that a retiree can withdraw 4% of their initial portfolio balance each year (adjusted for inflation) with a high probability of the portfolio lasting 30+ years.

- Retire with $1,000,000 → Withdraw $40,000/year
- Retire with $1,500,000 → Withdraw $60,000/year
- Retire with $2,000,000 → Withdraw $80,000/year

**Important caveat:** The 4% rule was developed for 30-year retirements using U.S. historical returns. For longer retirements (40+ years), many financial planners now recommend a 3%–3.5% withdrawal rate to increase longevity of the portfolio.

### How Much Do You Need to Retire?

A simple formula: **multiply your target annual income by 25**. This is the portfolio size that supports a 4% annual withdrawal indefinitely.

| Annual Income Needed | Target Portfolio |
|---------------------|-----------------|
| $40,000 | $1,000,000 |
| $60,000 | $1,500,000 |
| $80,000 | $2,000,000 |
| $100,000 | $2,500,000 |

These figures do not include Social Security income, pensions, or other guaranteed income sources. If you'll receive $20,000/year from Social Security, subtract that from your income need before applying the 25× rule.

### Tax-Advantaged Accounts — The Foundation

Most retirement savings flow through tax-advantaged accounts:

**401(k) / 403(b):** Employer-sponsored plans. 2024 contribution limit: $23,000 ($30,500 if age 50+). Contributions are pre-tax (traditional) or post-tax (Roth). Many employers match contributions — always contribute enough to capture the full match, as it is an immediate 50%–100% return.

**Traditional IRA:** Individual Retirement Account with pre-tax contributions. 2024 limit: $7,000 ($8,000 if age 50+). Deductibility phases out above certain income levels if you also have a workplace plan.

**Roth IRA:** Contributions are post-tax; qualified withdrawals in retirement are tax-free. Same contribution limits as Traditional IRA. Income limits apply to direct Roth IRA contributions.

Contribution limits are set by the IRS and adjust for inflation periodically. The calculator assumes your contribution includes both employee and employer amounts.

### Expected Rate of Return

The return assumption has an outsized effect on projected balances over long horizons. Standard assumptions used by financial planners:

- **Conservative:** 5%–6% (heavy bond allocation)
- **Moderate:** 6%–7% (balanced stock/bond mix)
- **Aggressive:** 8%–10% (heavy equity allocation)

These are nominal returns. For inflation-adjusted (real) returns, subtract approximately 3%. The S&P 500's historical inflation-adjusted average is approximately 7% annually over long periods, per Federal Reserve and academic data — but past performance does not guarantee future results.

### When to Start: The Cost of Waiting

Starting 10 years later roughly halves your ending balance. A 25-year-old saving $500/month at 7% for 40 years accumulates approximately **$1.32 million**. A 35-year-old doing the same for 30 years accumulates approximately **$605,000** — less than half. Time is your most irreplaceable asset in retirement planning.

### What This Calculator Doesn't Model

- **Social Security benefits** — estimate yours at the Social Security Administration's official website
- **Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs)** — the IRS requires withdrawals from traditional accounts starting at age 73
- **Healthcare costs** — often the largest unplanned expense in retirement; average healthcare costs for a 65-year-old couple in retirement have been estimated above $300,000 over the course of retirement
- **Tax treatment of withdrawals** — traditional account withdrawals are taxed as ordinary income; Roth withdrawals are not
