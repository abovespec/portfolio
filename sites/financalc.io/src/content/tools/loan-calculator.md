## Loan Calculator — Know Your Payment Before You Sign

Whether you're considering a personal loan, financing a car, or refinancing student debt, the single most important number is your monthly payment — and the total interest you'll pay over the loan's life. Our loan calculator gives you both in under five seconds, with no signup required.

### What You Can Calculate

Enter the **loan amount**, **annual interest rate (APR)**, and **loan term in months**. The calculator returns:

- **Monthly payment** — the fixed amount due each month
- **Total amount paid** — principal plus all interest over the full term
- **Total interest** — the cost of borrowing expressed in dollars
- **Amortization schedule** — showing principal and interest breakdown for every payment

### How Loan Payments Are Calculated

Most consumer loans use a fixed monthly payment calculated with the standard amortization formula:

**M = P × [r(1+r)^n] / [(1+r)^n − 1]**

Where:
- **M** = monthly payment
- **P** = principal (loan amount)
- **r** = monthly interest rate (APR ÷ 12 ÷ 100)
- **n** = total number of payments (term in months)

The payment stays the same every month, but the portion going to interest versus principal shifts over time. Early payments are mostly interest; later payments are mostly principal.

### Comparing Loan Scenarios

The calculator makes it easy to compare options side by side. A few illustrative examples:

| Loan Amount | APR | Term | Monthly Payment | Total Interest |
|-------------|-----|------|-----------------|----------------|
| $15,000 | 8% | 36 months | $470 | $1,921 |
| $15,000 | 8% | 60 months | $304 | $3,240 |
| $15,000 | 12% | 60 months | $333 | $5,000 |
| $30,000 | 6% | 72 months | $497 | $5,786 |

Key insight: **a longer term lowers your monthly payment but dramatically increases total interest paid.** A two-year extension on a $15,000 loan at 8% costs an additional $1,319 in interest.

### Types of Loans This Calculator Covers

**Personal loans** — typically unsecured, with fixed rates ranging from 6%–36% depending on creditworthiness. Terms of 12–84 months are common.

**Auto loans** — secured by the vehicle. Average new-car APR for borrowers with good credit hovers around 5%–8%; used-car loans typically run 1–3 percentage points higher. Terms of 36–84 months.

**Student loans** — federal student loan rates are set by Congress each year and published by the Department of Education. Private student loan rates vary widely.

**Home equity loans** — secured by your home equity. Typically lower rates than personal loans; treated like a second mortgage with fixed monthly payments.

### What APR Includes (and What It Doesn't)

The **APR (annual percentage rate)** includes the interest rate plus most fees the lender charges — origination fees, underwriting fees, and similar costs — expressed as a single annual percentage. It is a more complete picture of loan cost than the interest rate alone.

APR does not include optional costs like credit insurance, late payment fees, or prepayment penalties. Always read the loan agreement for the full fee schedule.

### Paying Off Your Loan Early

Most personal and auto loans allow early payoff without penalty (verify your contract). Paying more than the minimum payment each month accelerates principal reduction, lowering total interest substantially. Even one extra payment per year on a 60-month loan can cut months off the term.

Use the amortization table to see exactly how much interest is remaining at any point — that is the amount you save by paying off early.
