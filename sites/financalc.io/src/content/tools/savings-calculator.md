## Savings Calculator — Turn a Goal into a Plan

A savings goal without a plan is just a wish. Our savings calculator bridges the gap: enter what you have, what you can contribute, and what interest rate you expect to earn — and get a precise timeline and ending balance. Whether you're building an emergency fund, saving for a down payment, or setting aside money for a child's education, this tool turns abstract ambitions into concrete numbers.

### What This Calculator Models

Enter your **current savings**, **monthly contribution**, **annual interest rate**, and **time horizon** (or a **target balance** to work backward from). The calculator returns:

- **Projected balance** at your goal date
- **Months (or years) to reach your target** — if you set a goal amount
- **Total contributions** — dollars you actually deposited
- **Interest earned** — the free money compounding added
- **Year-by-year balance table**

### Savings Goal Mode vs. Time Mode

**Time mode:** "I'll save $300/month at 4.5% for 5 years — what do I end up with?" → The calculator projects your balance.

**Goal mode:** "I need $25,000 for a house down payment — how long will it take if I save $500/month at 4.5%?" → The calculator solves for time.

Both modes run in the same tool. Switch between them to plan for multiple goals simultaneously.

### What Interest Rate Should You Use?

Your rate depends on where you're saving:

| Account Type | Typical Rate (2025–2026) |
|--------------|--------------------------|
| Traditional savings account | 0.01%–0.5% |
| High-yield savings account (HYSA) | 4.0%–5.0% |
| Money market account | 3.5%–4.5% |
| 1-year CD | 4.5%–5.0% |
| 5-year CD | 3.5%–4.5% |

High-yield savings accounts currently offer returns that, for short-term goals, are competitive with many conservative investments. For goals 5+ years out, a tax-advantaged investment account (like a brokerage IRA) may produce higher returns — but with more volatility.

### Building an Emergency Fund

Financial professionals widely recommend maintaining 3–6 months of essential expenses in a liquid, accessible savings account. A household with $4,000/month in essential expenses needs $12,000–$24,000 in reserve.

If you have nothing saved today and put aside $400/month in a 4.5% HYSA, you'll reach a $12,000 emergency fund in approximately **29 months** — and a $24,000 fund in about **55 months**. The calculator lets you model any starting balance to see how quickly you can fill the gap.

### The 50/30/20 Rule and Savings Rate

A common personal finance guideline allocates after-tax income as:
- **50%** to needs (housing, food, utilities, transport)
- **30%** to wants (dining out, entertainment, subscriptions)
- **20%** to savings and debt repayment

If your monthly take-home is $5,000, the 20% target puts $1,000/month toward savings and debt payoff. Adjust the contribution input to model what your actual savings rate generates over different time horizons.

### Automating Your Savings

Behavioral economics research consistently shows that **automatic transfers** are more effective than manual saving. Setting up a recurring transfer the day after payday — before the money is available to spend — closes the gap between what people plan to save and what they actually save. Model your automatic contribution in this calculator to see the long-term impact.

### Multiple Goals at Once

Most people save for several goals simultaneously: emergency fund, vacation, down payment, retirement. Run the calculator once for each goal with its own target, timeline, and dedicated account to see whether your total monthly contribution is feasible within your budget.
