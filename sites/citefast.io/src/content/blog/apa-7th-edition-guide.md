The American Psychological Association published the 7th edition of its Publication Manual in October 2019, with it taking effect for most publications and course submissions in 2020. If your institution or instructor specifies "APA format," they almost certainly mean the 7th edition — the 6th edition (2009) is now outdated. This guide explains every significant change from the 6th edition and provides current rules for formatting your paper and citations.

## Why APA 7th edition matters

APA 7 is not a cosmetic update. It introduced meaningful changes to how authors are listed, how DOIs are formatted, how student papers differ from professional manuscripts, and how to use inclusive language. Using APA 6 rules in a paper that should follow APA 7 is a common source of lost marks.

## Change 1 — Running head

**APA 6:** A running head (an abbreviated title in all caps) was required in the header of every page, including student papers.

**APA 7:** The running head is required only for manuscripts being submitted for publication in a journal. Student papers do not use a running head. This is one of the most immediately visible changes.

**APA 6 header (student paper):**
```
Running head: SOCIAL MEDIA AND ADOLESCENT WELLBEING        1
```

**APA 7 header (student paper):**
```
                                                            1
```
Just the page number — no running head label, no abbreviated title.

## Change 2 — Author lists (the "et al." threshold)

**APA 6:** List up to six authors. With seven or more, list the first six followed by an ellipsis and the final author.

**APA 7:** List up to 20 authors. With 21 or more, list the first 19, then an ellipsis (`...`), then the final author. The "et al." threshold for in-text citations also changed.

**In-text citation threshold:**

| Number of authors | APA 6 | APA 7 |
|-------------------|-------|-------|
| 1 author | (Smith, 2020) | (Smith, 2020) |
| 2 authors | (Smith & Jones, 2020) | (Smith & Jones, 2020) |
| 3 authors | (Smith, Jones, & Davis, 2020) — first time | (Smith et al., 2020) |
| 4–5 authors | (Smith et al., 2020) after first use | (Smith et al., 2020) |
| 6+ authors | (Smith et al., 2020) | (Smith et al., 2020) |

In APA 7, three or more authors are condensed to "et al." from the very first citation. In APA 6, you listed all authors (up to five) on first mention.

**APA 6 first citation (three authors):**
```
(Smith, Jones, & Davis, 2020)
```

**APA 7 first citation (three authors):**
```
(Smith et al., 2020)
```

## Change 3 — DOI format

**APA 6:** DOIs were formatted using the `doi:` prefix without a hyperlink.

**APA 7:** DOIs are formatted as full HTTPS hyperlinks.

**APA 6 format:**
```
doi:10.1037/emo0000504
```

**APA 7 format:**
```
https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0000504
```

This applies consistently to all source types: journal articles, books, reports, datasets. If a DOI exists, use the `https://doi.org/` format.

## Change 4 — Publisher location omitted for books

**APA 6:** Book references required the publisher's city and state (or city and country for international publishers).

**APA 7:** Publisher location is omitted entirely.

**APA 6 book reference:**
```
Kahneman, D. (2011). Thinking, fast and slow. New York, NY: Farrar, Straus and Giroux.
```

**APA 7 book reference:**
```
Kahneman, D. (2011). Thinking, fast and slow. Farrar, Straus and Giroux.
```

## Change 5 — Website citations simplified

**APA 6:** Website citations often required stating that the source was retrieved from a website.

**APA 7:** The retrieval statement is gone. Simply list the URL at the end.

**APA 6:**
```
Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. (2020). About COVID-19. Retrieved from https://www.cdc.gov/coronavirus/2019-ncov/about/index.html
```

**APA 7:**
```
Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. (2020). About COVID-19. https://www.cdc.gov/coronavirus/2019-ncov/about/index.html
```

For most websites, you no longer include "Retrieved from." The exception is sources that change over time (like wikis), where you may add a retrieval date.

## Change 6 — Student vs professional paper distinction

APA 7 formally distinguishes between two paper types:

**Student papers** include:
- Title page with: paper title, author name(s), affiliation (course number, course name, instructor name, due date)
- No running head
- No author note

**Professional papers** (for journal submission) include:
- Running head
- Author note (disclosures, funding, contact info)
- Structured abstract with keywords

Your instructor will specify which format to use. Most course assignments use the student paper format.

## Change 7 — Heading levels clarified

APA 7 updated the visual formatting of headings. All five heading levels now include text on the same line as the paragraph beginning (in some cases), unlike APA 6 where lower-level headings had a different indentation pattern.

| Level | APA 7 format |
|-------|-------------|
| 1 | **Bold, Centered, Title Case** |
| 2 | **Bold, Left-Aligned, Title Case** |
| 3 | **Bold, Italic, Left-Aligned, Title Case** |
| 4 | **Bold, Indented, Title Case, ends with period.** Text begins on same line. |
| 5 | **Bold, Italic, Indented, Title Case, ends with period.** Text begins on same line. |

## Change 8 — Bias-free language guidelines expanded

APA 7 significantly expanded its guidance on inclusive, bias-free language. Key points:

- **Gender:** Use gender-neutral language where possible. "They/them" as a singular pronoun is explicitly endorsed. Avoid "he or she" constructions. Use "people with schizophrenia" not "schizophrenics."
- **Race and ethnicity:** Capitalize racial and ethnic group names (Black, White, Indigenous, Asian American). Avoid "non-White" as a term — specify the group.
- **Age:** Use "older adults" rather than "the elderly" or "seniors."
- **Disability:** Use person-first language (person with a disability) unless the community prefers identity-first language (autistic person — which many in the autism community prefer). Follow community norms.
- **Socioeconomic status:** Avoid "low-income people" as a vague shorthand; be specific.

These are not optional stylistic preferences — they are requirements in APA 7.

## Change 9 — Abbreviations for common reference types

APA 7 added some new abbreviations for clarity in reference entries:

- `Ed.` / `Eds.` for editor(s)
- `Trans.` for translator
- `Vol.` for volume of a multivolume work
- `No.` for report numbers (government/tech reports)

These were present in APA 6 but are more consistently applied in APA 7.

## Change 10 — Removed "place of publication" for databases

In APA 6, some database sources required a retrieval statement naming the database. In APA 7, you generally do not name the database for articles — you include the DOI or URL. Name the database only if the work cannot be found elsewhere (e.g., APA PsycTests for psychological tests, or proprietary datasets).

## APA 7 reference examples for common source types

### Journal article with DOI

```
Grady, J. S., Her, M., Moreno, G., Perez, C., & Yelinek, J. (2019). Emotions in storybooks. Emotion, 19(7), 1172–1191. https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0000504
```

### Book

```
Strunk, W., Jr., & White, E. B. (1999). The elements of style (4th ed.). Longman.
```

### Chapter in an edited book

```
Bjork, R. A. (1989). Retrieval inhibition as an adaptive mechanism in human memory. In H. L. Roediger & F. I. M. Craik (Eds.), Varieties of memory and consciousness (pp. 309–330). Erlbaum.
```

### Webpage

```
World Health Organization. (2026, February 10). Depression fact sheet. https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/depression
```

### Social media post (Twitter/X)

```
Obama, B. [@BarackObama]. (2020, July 30). Today, I'm excited to share some of the first folks who will receive support through the Obama Foundation's... [Tweet]. Twitter. https://twitter.com/BarackObama/status/1288854965514338304
```

### YouTube video

```
TED. (2023, March 14). The mathematics of love | Hannah Fry [Video]. YouTube. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yFVXsjVdvmY
```

## Summary of APA 7 vs APA 6 changes

| Rule | APA 6 | APA 7 |
|------|-------|-------|
| Running head | Required for all papers | Only for manuscripts for publication |
| Author limit (reference) | Up to 6, then et al. | Up to 20, then ... last author |
| et al. threshold (in-text) | 6+ authors | 3+ authors |
| DOI format | doi:10.xxx | https://doi.org/10.xxx |
| Publisher location | Required | Omitted |
| "Retrieved from" | Often required | Omitted for most sources |
| Student/professional distinction | Not formalized | Explicitly distinct formats |
| Singular "they" | Not endorsed | Explicitly endorsed |
| Racial group capitalization | Inconsistent | Consistently capitalized |

APA 7 rules are now well-established and expected at most universities. If you have papers from before 2020, they likely follow APA 6 and may need updating for current submissions.
