Journal articles are among the most cited source types in academic writing. Whether you are submitting a psychology paper, a literature essay, or a biomedical report, you will almost certainly need to cite at least one peer-reviewed article. The challenge is that the format differs significantly between APA, MLA, and Chicago — and even within those styles there are variations for print versus online sources, sources with DOIs versus those with URLs, and sources with missing information like volume or issue numbers.

This guide walks through each style with templates and concrete examples so you can format journal citations correctly every time.

## What information you need before you start

Before formatting the citation, locate the following details from the article:

1. **Author(s)** — last name and initials (APA/Chicago) or full name (MLA)
2. **Publication year** — the year the article was published in that volume
3. **Article title** — the exact title of the article itself
4. **Journal name** — the full official name of the journal
5. **Volume number** — the annual volume the issue belongs to
6. **Issue number** — the specific issue within that volume
7. **Page range** — the first and last page of the article (e.g., 112–134)
8. **DOI** — Digital Object Identifier (preferred over URL when available)
9. **URL** — only needed if there is no DOI

Most of this information appears on the first page of the article or in the journal's table of contents. Database records on Google Scholar, PubMed, or JSTOR also display all of these fields.

## APA 7th edition journal article citation

APA style is the standard in psychology, social sciences, education, and nursing. The 7th edition (2020) is the current version.

### APA template — print or online with DOI

```
Author, A. A., & Author, B. B. (Year). Title of article. Journal Name, Volume(Issue), Page–Page. https://doi.org/xxxxx
```

Key formatting rules:
- Author initials only (not full first names)
- Article title: sentence case (only first word, proper nouns, and first word after colon capitalized)
- Journal name: title case, italicized
- Volume number: italicized
- Issue number: in parentheses, not italicized
- DOI formatted as a full hyperlink: `https://doi.org/10.xxxx`

### APA example — two authors with DOI

```
Grady, J. S., & Her, M. (2019). Emotions in storybooks: A content analysis of picture books for young children. Emotion, 19(7), 1172–1191. https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0000504
```

### APA example — six authors with DOI

When there are three or more authors, list all authors up to 20. If there are 21 or more, list the first 19, then an ellipsis, then the final author.

```
Wampold, B. E., Minami, T., Tierney, S. C., Baskin, T. W., & Bhati, K. S. (2005). The placebo is powerful: Estimating placebo effects in medicine and psychotherapy from randomized clinical trials. Journal of Clinical Psychology, 61(7), 835–854. https://doi.org/10.1002/jclp.20129
```

### APA example — article with no DOI, found online

If an article has no DOI but is freely available online, include the URL. If it comes from a library database without a stable URL, omit the URL entirely.

```
Weir, K. (2017, January). Forgiveness can improve mental and physical health. Monitor on Psychology, 47(1). https://www.apa.org/monitor/2017/01/ce-corner
```

Note: When there are no page numbers (as with online-only articles), simply omit them.

### APA example — print journal, no DOI

```
Hambrick, D. Z., & Meinz, E. J. (2011). Limits on the predictive power of domain-specific experience and knowledge in skilled performance. Current Directions in Psychological Science, 20(5), 275–279.
```

### APA in-text citation for a journal article

```
(Grady & Her, 2019)
(Wampold et al., 2005)
```

For a direct quote, add the page number:

```
(Grady & Her, 2019, p. 1175)
```

## MLA 9th edition journal article citation

MLA style is standard in English, literature, humanities, and cultural studies.

### MLA template

```
Author Last, First, and First Last. "Title of Article." Journal Name, vol. #, no. #, Year, pp. #–#. DOI or URL.
```

Key formatting rules:
- Full first names used
- Article title in quotation marks, title case
- Journal name italicized, title case
- Volume and issue written as `vol. 14, no. 3`
- Pages written as `pp. 112–134`
- DOI or URL at the end (if accessed online)

### MLA example — single author with DOI

```
Hambrick, David Z. "The Myth of Innate Talent." Scientific American Mind, vol. 25, no. 5, 2014, pp. 36–41. https://doi.org/10.1038/scientificamericanmind0914-36.
```

### MLA example — two authors, print journal

```
Grady, Jessica S., and Minhee Her. "Emotions in Storybooks: A Content Analysis of Picture Books for Young Children." Emotion, vol. 19, no. 7, 2019, pp. 1172–1191.
```

### MLA example — article from a database

When you access an article through a library database like JSTOR or EBSCOhost, include the database name in italics and the DOI or URL:

```
Smith, Zadie. "Two Directions for the Novel." Changing My Mind, Penguin, 2009. JSTOR, www.jstor.org/stable/10.2307/j.ctt18fs7d9.
```

### MLA in-text citation for a journal article

MLA uses author-page number format:

```
(Hambrick 38)
(Grady and Her 1175)
```

If the article has no page numbers (online only):

```
(Hambrick)
```

## Chicago style journal article citation

Chicago has two documentation systems. Notes-Bibliography (N-B) is used in history and humanities. Author-Date is used in social and physical sciences.

### Chicago Notes-Bibliography — footnote

```
1. First A. Last, "Title of Article," Journal Name Volume, no. Issue (Year): Page.
```

**Example:**

```
1. David Z. Hambrick and Elizabeth J. Meinz, "Limits on the Predictive Power of Domain-Specific Experience and Knowledge," Current Directions in Psychological Science 20, no. 5 (2011): 277.
```

### Chicago Notes-Bibliography — bibliography entry

```
Last, First A. "Title of Article." Journal Name Volume, no. Issue (Year): Pages. https://doi.org/xxxxx.
```

**Example:**

```
Hambrick, David Z., and Elizabeth J. Meinz. "Limits on the Predictive Power of Domain-Specific Experience and Knowledge in Skilled Performance." Current Directions in Psychological Science 20, no. 5 (2011): 275–279.
```

### Chicago Author-Date — in-text citation

```
(Hambrick and Meinz 2011, 277)
```

### Chicago Author-Date — reference list entry

```
Hambrick, David Z., and Elizabeth J. Meinz. 2011. "Limits on the Predictive Power of Domain-Specific Experience and Knowledge in Skilled Performance." Current Directions in Psychological Science 20 (5): 275–279.
```

## Handling DOIs

A DOI (Digital Object Identifier) is a permanent link that reliably resolves to the article even if the URL changes. Always prefer a DOI over a URL.

- **APA format:** `https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0000504`
- **MLA format:** `https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0000504.` (period at end of entry)
- **Chicago format:** `https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0000504.` (period at end)

If you only have the raw DOI (e.g., `10.1037/emo0000504`), prepend `https://doi.org/` to make it a working link.

## Online versus print journals

The same article may be available in print and online. The key differences:

| Element | Print | Online (with DOI) | Online (no DOI) |
|---------|-------|-------------------|-----------------|
| Page numbers | Always include | Include if available | Omit if unavailable |
| DOI | N/A | Always include | N/A |
| URL | N/A | Not needed if DOI present | Include if available |
| Access date | Not needed | Not needed (APA/MLA) | Not needed (APA) / Optional (MLA) |

## Missing information — what to do

**No volume or issue number:** Omit the missing element and include whatever is available. Some online-first articles are published ahead of their print volume.

**No page numbers:** Omit page numbers. For APA in-text quotes, you may use a paragraph number: `(Smith, 2024, para. 4)`.

**Article not in English:** APA requires you to include the original title followed by an English translation in brackets. Chicago and MLA allow similar treatment.

**Advance online publication:** APA uses `Advance online publication` in place of the volume/issue/page data:

```
Nguyen, T. (2026). Stress and academic performance in first-year students. Journal of Educational Psychology. Advance online publication. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000000
```
