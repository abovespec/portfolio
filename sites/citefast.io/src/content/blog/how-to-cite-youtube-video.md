YouTube videos are a legitimate source for many types of academic and professional writing — whether you are citing a TED talk, a government press briefing, a documentary excerpt, a tutorial, or a primary-source speech. All three major citation styles (APA, MLA, and Chicago) have specific formats for online video content. Getting the details right requires understanding how to identify the "author" of a YouTube video, how to format the video title, and how to handle special cases like timestamps and channels that have since been taken down.

## What information to collect before you cite

Before formatting the citation, gather the following from the YouTube video page:

1. **Uploader name** — the channel or person who uploaded the video (visible under the title)
2. **Username** — the channel handle (e.g., `@TED`, `@veritasium`) — used by some styles
3. **Upload date** — shown on the video page (e.g., "Jun 3, 2025")
4. **Video title** — the exact title as shown on the page
5. **Platform** — YouTube
6. **URL** — the full video URL (e.g., `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XXXXXXXXXXX`)
7. **Timestamp** — if you are citing a specific moment, note the time (e.g., 3:42)
8. **Duration** — required by some style variations, optional in others

The uploader is not always the original creator. For example, a TED conference channel uploads talks by individual speakers. For most academic citation purposes, you use the **name as it appears on the channel** — the uploader — unless there is a named speaker or presenter who is the genuine author of the content.

## APA 7th edition YouTube citation

### APA template

```
Last, F. [Username]. (Year, Month Day). Title of video [Video]. YouTube. URL
```

Or, if the channel name is already descriptive and matches the real name:

```
Channel Name. (Year, Month Day). Title of video [Video]. YouTube. URL
```

### Key APA formatting rules

- The uploader's real name goes first if known, followed by their username in brackets
- If the channel name and real name are the same, use just the name without brackets
- The video title is in italics, sentence case
- Include `[Video]` in brackets after the title to identify the format
- The source is `YouTube.`
- End with the full URL

### APA examples

**Example — individual creator, channel name is real name:**
```
Sanderson, G. (2022, September 23). But what is a neural network? [Video]. YouTube. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aircAruvnKk
```

**Example — channel name is a handle/alias, real name known:**
```
Muller, D. [Veritasium]. (2021, May 22). The simplest math problem no one can solve [Video]. YouTube. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=094y1Z2wpJg
```

**Example — channel is an organization (no individual author):**
```
TED. (2023, March 14). The mathematics of love | Hannah Fry [Video]. YouTube. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yFVXsjVdvmY
```

**Example — government or institutional channel:**
```
NASA Goddard. (2024, July 4). Webb telescope observes Uranus and its 13 rings [Video]. YouTube. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XXXXXXXXX
```

**Example — news organization clip:**
```
The Guardian. (2025, January 20). President's inauguration address — full speech [Video]. YouTube. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XXXXXXXXX
```

### APA in-text citations for YouTube videos

```
(Sanderson, 2022)
(Muller, 2021)
(TED, 2023)
(NASA Goddard, 2024)
```

### Citing a specific moment in the video (timestamp)

If you are paraphrasing or quoting a specific section of the video, include the timestamp in your in-text citation the way you would use a page number:

```
(TED, 2023, 4:32)
```

This means you are referencing what was said or shown at the 4-minute, 32-second mark.

## MLA 9th edition YouTube citation

### MLA template

```
Last, First, or Channel Name. "Title of Video." YouTube, Day Month Year, URL.
```

### Key MLA formatting rules

- Video title in quotation marks, title case
- `YouTube` in italics as the container
- Upload date in Day Month Year order (abbreviated month)
- URL at the end without angle brackets

### MLA examples

**Example — individual creator:**
```
Sanderson, Grant. "But What Is a Neural Network?" YouTube, 23 Sept. 2022, www.youtube.com/watch?v=aircAruvnKk.
```

**Example — channel/alias as author:**
```
Veritasium. "The Simplest Math Problem No One Can Solve." YouTube, 22 May 2021, www.youtube.com/watch?v=094y1Z2wpJg.
```

**Example — organizational channel:**
```
TED. "The Mathematics of Love | Hannah Fry." YouTube, 14 Mar. 2023, www.youtube.com/watch?v=yFVXsjVdvmY.
```

**Example — if you want to credit the speaker in the title:**
```
Fry, Hannah. "The Mathematics of Love." YouTube, uploaded by TED, 14 Mar. 2023, www.youtube.com/watch?v=yFVXsjVdvmY.
```

This second MLA form is useful when the actual content creator (a speaker, a filmmaker) is distinct from the uploader (the channel).

### MLA in-text citation for YouTube videos

MLA uses author-page format. Since videos have no page numbers, cite by author name only:

```
(Sanderson)
(TED)
```

### Citing a timestamp in MLA

When referencing a specific point, include the timestamp after the author name in-text:

```
(TED 4:32)
```

## Chicago style YouTube citation

Chicago has two systems. Notes-Bibliography (N-B) is used in the humanities; Author-Date is used in social sciences. Online video citations work slightly differently in each.

### Chicago Notes-Bibliography — footnote

```
1. Channel Name or First Last, "Title of Video," YouTube video, Duration, Month Day, Year, URL.
```

**Example:**
```
1. TED, "The Mathematics of Love | Hannah Fry," YouTube video, 17:28, March 14, 2023, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yFVXsjVdvmY.
```

**Example — individual creator:**
```
2. Grant Sanderson, "But What Is a Neural Network?" YouTube video, 19:13, September 23, 2022, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aircAruvnKk.
```

### Chicago Notes-Bibliography — bibliography entry

```
Channel Name or Last, First. "Title of Video." YouTube video. Duration. Month Day, Year. URL.
```

**Example:**
```
TED. "The Mathematics of Love | Hannah Fry." YouTube video. 17:28. March 14, 2023. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yFVXsjVdvmY.
```

**Example — individual:**
```
Sanderson, Grant. "But What Is a Neural Network?" YouTube video. 19:13. September 23, 2022. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aircAruvnKk.
```

### Chicago Author-Date — in-text citation

```
(TED 2023)
(Sanderson 2022)
```

### Chicago Author-Date — reference list

```
TED. 2023. "The Mathematics of Love | Hannah Fry." YouTube video, 17:28. March 14, 2023. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yFVXsjVdvmY.
```

## Channel as author versus individual creator

This is the most common point of confusion. Apply these guidelines:

- **Use the channel name** when the channel is the clear author of the content (a news clip, an organization's official statement, a conference recording)
- **Use the individual's name** when a specific person is the creator who happens to upload to their own channel (a vlogger, a professor, a musician)
- **Credit both** (in MLA style) when the speaker is distinct from the channel — e.g., a TED speaker on the TED channel

When in doubt, use the channel name. This is consistent with how APA 7 and most citation guides handle corporate or institutional authorship.

## How to handle unavailable or deleted videos

If you viewed a video that has since been taken down, you still cite it as you found it — using the details from your viewing. Note that the video may no longer be accessible by adding `[Video no longer available]` after the URL.

**APA:**
```
Channel Name. (Year, Month Day). Title of video [Video]. YouTube. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XXXXXXXXX [Video no longer available]
```

If you never saw the video yourself and are citing it from a secondary description, you should find the original source and cite that instead, or note that the information comes from a secondary reference.

Alternatively, check the Internet Archive's Wayback Machine or specialized video archives, as some deleted YouTube videos are preserved there.

## Special cases

### Playlist citation

If you are citing a playlist as a whole rather than a specific video, use the playlist URL and describe the format accordingly.

**APA:**
```
Khan Academy. (n.d.). Algebra basics [Video playlist]. YouTube. https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLSQl0a2vh4HC5feHa6Rc5c0wbRTx56nF7
```

### Live stream

For a live stream that was later archived on YouTube:

**APA:**
```
NASA. (2025, July 16). Artemis IV launch — live coverage [Video]. YouTube. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XXXXXXXXX
```

### Video with a description in the title

Some YouTube titles include editorial context added by the uploader. Reproduce the title as-is.

## Quick comparison table

| Element | APA | MLA | Chicago N-B footnote |
|---------|-----|-----|----------------------|
| Author | Last, F. [Username] | Last, First | First Last or Channel Name |
| Title | Italics, sentence case | Quotes, title case | Quotes, title case |
| Format tag | [Video] after title | Not required | "YouTube video" |
| Date | (Year, Month Day) | Day Month Year | Month Day, Year |
| Duration | Not required | Not required | Included |
| URL | https://... | www.youtube.com/... | https://... |
| In-text | (Author, Year) | (Author) | Footnote number |
