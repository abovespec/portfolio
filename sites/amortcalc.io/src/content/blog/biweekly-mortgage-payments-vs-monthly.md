When you take out a 30-year mortgage, you make 12 payments a year — one per month — for a total of 360 payments. A biweekly payment plan sounds similar, but the math works out differently: you make a payment every two weeks instead of every month. The result is **26 half-payments per year**, which equals **13 full monthly payments** rather than 12.

That one extra payment per year, applied entirely to principal, is enough to cut several years off a typical mortgage and save tens of thousands of dollars in interest.

## Why Biweekly Produces an Extra Payment

There are 52 weeks in a year. Divide by two and you get 26 biweekly periods. Each period you pay half your normal monthly payment, so:

> 26 half-payments = 13 full monthly payments

Since your loan is structured around 12 monthly payments, that 13th payment is pure bonus — it goes directly to principal every year. No negotiation required, no lump sum needed; the extra payment emerges automatically from the calendar math.

## The Numbers: A $350,000 Loan at 6.5%

*These figures are illustrative. Actual savings depend on your loan balance, interest rate, and when biweekly payments begin.*

**Monthly payment:** $2,212.24  
**Half-payment (biweekly amount):** $1,106.12

### Monthly payment schedule (baseline)

- Total payments: 360
- Total paid: $796,406
- Total interest: $446,406

### Biweekly payment schedule

- Loan paid off in approximately: **25 years 9 months** (instead of 30)
- Total payments: approximately 670 half-payments
- Total paid: approximately $740,000
- Total interest: approximately **$390,000**
- **Interest saved: approximately $56,000**
- **Time saved: approximately 4 years 3 months**

The savings are significant — but they come entirely from that one extra full payment per year, not from any magic in the biweekly timing itself.

## The Equivalent DIY Approach

You do not necessarily need a formal biweekly program to capture these savings. The identical result can be achieved by:

**Option A — Divide by 12:** Add 1/12 of your monthly payment to every regular payment as extra principal.

For the $350,000 example: $2,212.24 ÷ 12 = **$184.35 extra per month**

Every month you pay $2,212.24 + $184.35 = $2,396.59. Over a year that equals exactly one extra payment, and the savings match the biweekly approach almost perfectly.

**Option B — One annual lump sum:** Make one extra full mortgage payment each year, applied to principal. This is mathematically equivalent and gives you flexibility about timing (tax refund, bonus, etc.).

Either approach delivers the same result as biweekly payments without the need to enroll in a special program.

## Biweekly Programs: What to Watch For

Many banks, mortgage servicers, and third-party companies offer biweekly payment plans — sometimes for a fee. Before enrolling, ask these questions:

**1. Is there an enrollment fee?**
Some third-party services charge $200–$400 to set up biweekly payments, plus a monthly processing fee. Given that the DIY version costs nothing, fees reduce or eliminate the net benefit for years.

**2. When does the servicer apply the extra funds?**
Some servicers hold your biweekly half-payment in a suspense account and only apply it to your loan when the second half arrives — effectively making it a monthly payment with no interest benefit. A genuine biweekly program applies each half-payment to your balance immediately.

**3. Does extra principal go directly to the balance?**
Confirm in writing that the 13th payment each year is applied to principal, not held for the next month's payment.

The CFPB advises borrowers to review their monthly mortgage statement to verify that extra payments are being applied as intended.

## When Biweekly Payments Make the Most Sense

Biweekly payments are most effective when:

- **You are early in your loan.** The earlier extra principal is applied, the more future interest it eliminates. Starting biweekly payments in Year 1 saves far more than starting in Year 15.
- **Your rate is high.** A higher interest rate means each extra dollar of principal eliminates more interest. The savings are proportionally larger on a 7% loan than a 4% loan.
- **Your budget naturally aligns with biweekly pay cycles.** Many people are paid every two weeks. Aligning mortgage payments with paydays simplifies budgeting and reduces the risk of missed payments.

## When Monthly Payments May Be Preferable

Biweekly is not always the right choice:

- **If you have high-interest debt**, such as credit card balances, the extra mortgage principal payment may produce less financial benefit than eliminating that debt first.
- **If your emergency fund is thin**, locking in higher effective monthly payments reduces liquidity. Some financial planners suggest fully funding an emergency reserve before accelerating mortgage payoff.
- **If you plan to move or refinance within a few years**, the savings from accelerated payoff may not materialize.

## Calculating Your Own Savings

The savings from any extra-payment strategy depend on three variables: your current balance, your interest rate, and the size of the extra payment. The biweekly strategy is one of the most straightforward ways to reduce the total cost of a mortgage without refinancing.
