# GPA Scale 4.0 Explained: Letter Grades, Plus/Minus, and Weighted vs Unweighted

The 4.0 GPA scale is the standard measurement of academic performance used by nearly every high school and college in the United States. If you have ever wondered exactly how an A becomes a 4.0, or why your GPA looks different on different reports, this guide covers it completely.

## The Core 4.0 Scale

At its simplest, the GPA scale maps letter grades to numeric points:

| Letter Grade | GPA Points |
|---|---|
| A | 4.0 |
| B | 3.0 |
| C | 2.0 |
| D | 1.0 |
| F | 0.0 |

Your GPA is the average of those point values, weighted by the credit hours of each course. A 3-credit course has three times the impact of a 1-credit course.

## Plus and Minus Variations

Most schools use plus and minus grades, which add finer steps to the scale:

| Letter Grade | GPA Points |
|---|---|
| A+ | 4.0 (sometimes 4.3) |
| A | 4.0 |
| A− | 3.7 |
| B+ | 3.3 |
| B | 3.0 |
| B− | 2.7 |
| C+ | 2.3 |
| C | 2.0 |
| C− | 1.7 |
| D+ | 1.3 |
| D | 1.0 |
| D− | 0.7 |
| F | 0.0 |

For more on this topic, see [*Letter Grade to GPA Conversion Chart: The Complete Reference*](/blog/letter-grade-to-gpa-conversion-chart).

A few things worth noting:

- **A+ treatment varies.** Some schools cap A+ at 4.0. Others give 4.3, which can push a weighted average above 4.0 on an unweighted scale.
- **D− is usually still passing.** Most schools count any grade above F as credit, though policies differ on whether a D earns major credit.
- **Some schools don't use plus/minus at all.** In that case, any grade in the A range is exactly 4.0.

## How Your GPA Is Actually Calculated

To compute GPA manually:

1. Multiply each course's GPA points by its credit hours to get quality points.
2. Sum all quality points.
3. Divide by total credit hours.

**Example:**

| Course | Grade | GPA Points | Credits | Quality Points |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Calculus | B+ | 3.3 | 4 | 13.2 |
| History | A− | 3.7 | 3 | 11.1 |
| Chemistry | B | 3.0 | 4 | 12.0 |
| English | A | 4.0 | 3 | 12.0 |

Total quality points: 48.3 — Total credits: 14 — **GPA: 48.3 ÷ 14 = 3.45**

## Unweighted GPA

An unweighted GPA treats every course the same regardless of difficulty. An A in a remedial class gives the same 4.0 as an A in a college-level honors course. The maximum unweighted GPA is 4.0.

Colleges use unweighted GPA as one way to compare students across schools with different grading philosophies. It answers the question: "Did this student earn high grades?" without factoring in course difficulty.

## Weighted GPA

A weighted GPA gives extra points for harder courses. The most common boosts are:

| Course Type | Grade Bonus |
|---|---|
| Honors | +0.5 |
| AP (Advanced Placement) | +1.0 |
| IB (International Baccalaureate) | +1.0 |
| Dual Enrollment | +0.5 to +1.0 |

Under this system, an A in an AP class earns 5.0 points instead of 4.0. A weighted GPA can exceed 4.0, which is why you sometimes see high school GPAs of 4.5 or higher.

Weighted GPAs answer a different question: "Did this student challenge themselves and still perform well?"

## Which GPA Do Colleges Care About?

Both, but differently:

- **Admissions** — Most selective colleges recalculate your GPA using their own formula to neutralize school-to-school differences in weighting. Do not assume a high weighted GPA automatically impresses them.
- **Class rank** — Often calculated on weighted GPA, which rewards students who take rigorous courses.
- **Scholarships** — Merit scholarships usually specify a minimum unweighted GPA, often 3.0 or 3.5.
- **Graduate school** — Almost always looks at unweighted GPA on a 4.0 scale.

## Common Misconceptions

**"I need a 4.0 to get into a good college."** Most highly selective schools have median GPAs in the 3.7–3.9 range. A 4.0 is impressive but not the only path.

**"My weighted GPA is what matters."** A 4.5 weighted GPA built on inflated grades at a school with loose standards may impress less than a 3.8 unweighted GPA from a rigorous program.

**"F grades disappear if I retake the course."** Policies vary widely. Some schools replace the F; others average both attempts. Know your school's policy before retaking a course.

## The Bottom Line

The 4.0 scale converts letter grades to a uniform numeric system. Unweighted GPA caps at 4.0 and treats all courses equally. Weighted GPA goes higher by rewarding course rigor. Use the [grade calculator](/) to track where you stand and model what grades you need to hit your target GPA.
