# What Is a Good GPA in College? Context by Major, Grad School, and Employers

"Is my GPA good?" is one of the most common academic questions — and the most context-dependent. A 3.2 can be excellent in one field and a liability in another. This guide breaks down what GPA benchmarks actually mean across different situations.

## The Baseline: What GPA Numbers Signify

Before context, here is what the raw numbers mean on a 4.0 scale:

| GPA Range | Letter Grade Equivalent | General Standing |
|---|---|---|
| 3.9–4.0 | Mostly As | Exceptional |
| 3.5–3.89 | Mostly As with some B+s | Very strong |
| 3.0–3.49 | Mix of As and Bs | Solid/good |
| 2.5–2.99 | Mostly Bs and Cs | Adequate |
| 2.0–2.49 | Mostly Cs | Below average |
| Below 2.0 | D range | Academic risk |

Most colleges require a 2.0 minimum to remain enrolled. Many require 2.0 in your major courses as well.

## GPA by Major: Grade Distributions Are Not Equal

One of the most important things to understand is that grading standards vary dramatically across disciplines. A 3.5 in engineering means something different than a 3.5 in education.

### Majors with Tighter Grade Distributions

In STEM fields and pre-professional programs, grade distributions tend to be compressed. Curves are common, but the median grade is often a B or B−.

| Major Type | Typical Strong GPA | Context |
|---|---|---|
| Engineering | 3.2–3.5 | High difficulty, compressed curves |
| Chemistry / Physics | 3.2–3.5 | Weed-out courses pull GPA down early |
| Computer Science | 3.3–3.7 | Varies by program rigor |
| Pre-Med (Biology) | 3.5+ recommended for med school | Strict GPA thresholds in applications |
| Nursing | 3.0–3.5 | Competitive programs have cutoffs |

### Majors with Higher Average GPAs

Some fields have more generous grading norms, which means higher GPAs are more common and expected.

| Major Type | Typical Strong GPA | Context |
|---|---|---|
| Education | 3.5–3.9 | Grades tend to skew higher |
| Humanities (English, History) | 3.4–3.8 | Subjective grading, generally generous |
| Business | 3.3–3.7 | Grade inflation common at many schools |
| Social Work | 3.4–3.8 | Practice-focused, less curve pressure |

The point is not that humanities students work less hard — it is that your GPA should be interpreted relative to what is typical in your field.

## What GPA Do You Need for Graduate School?

Graduate school thresholds vary significantly by program type and selectivity.

### Medical School (MD)

The average GPA for medical school matriculants at allopathic (MD) schools is approximately 3.7. Osteopathic (DO) schools average slightly lower, around 3.5. A GPA below 3.0 makes admission extremely difficult at any accredited program.

### Law School

Top 14 law schools (T14) typically have median GPAs of 3.7–3.9. Regional law schools may accept students with GPAs as low as 3.0. The LSAT is equally important, but GPA below 3.5 limits options at elite schools.

### MBA Programs

Top MBA programs (Harvard, Stanford, Wharton) have median GPAs of 3.6–3.8. Most programs weigh work experience heavily, so a lower GPA (3.0–3.3) can be offset by exceptional professional accomplishments.

### PhD Programs

Requirements vary by field, but most PhD programs want at least a 3.3, with competitive programs expecting 3.5–3.7 in your major courses. Research experience and letters of recommendation often matter more than GPA for PhD admissions.

### Master's Programs

Professional master's degrees (MS, MA, MFA) vary widely. Many state university programs accept students with a 3.0. Competitive programs at elite universities want 3.5 or above.

**Summary table:**

| Program Type | Minimum Competitive GPA | Strong Application GPA |
|---|---|---|
| MD (allopathic) | 3.5 | 3.7+ |
| DO (osteopathic) | 3.3 | 3.5+ |
| Top law schools | 3.5 | 3.7+ |
| Regional law schools | 3.0 | 3.4+ |
| Top MBA programs | 3.3 | 3.6+ |
| PhD programs | 3.3 | 3.5–3.7 |
| Master's programs | 3.0 | 3.5+ |

## What GPA Do Employers Care About?

The answer depends heavily on the industry and company.

### Industries with Hard GPA Screens

**Consulting, investment banking, and finance** at top firms commonly filter resumes at 3.5, with some firms using 3.7 as the cutoff for on-campus recruiting. If your GPA is below the screen, your resume may not reach a human reader.

**Big Law** (large law firms) screens heavily by GPA and law school prestige. Top firms want law school GPAs in the top third of the class.

**Engineering and tech companies** vary. Some large firms (certain defense contractors, legacy tech) screen at 3.0–3.2. Others (many software companies) barely weigh GPA after the first job.

### Industries Where GPA Matters Less

After a few years of work experience, GPA fades in relevance for most industries. Employers start caring about:
- Portfolio of work (design, writing, code)
- Projects and internships
- Demonstrated skills
- Professional references

In creative fields, startups, and many tech roles, a strong portfolio can matter more than GPA at any stage.

### Practical Thresholds to Know

| GPA | Employer Situation |
|---|---|
| 3.7+ | Can list confidently anywhere; opens all screened roles |
| 3.5–3.69 | Good for most employers; may be just below some elite screens |
| 3.0–3.49 | Acceptable for most roles; focus on experience and projects |
| Below 3.0 | Leave off resume for screened roles; let experience lead |

When your GPA is below 3.0, many career advisors recommend simply omitting it from your resume and focusing on skills and accomplishments.
