# Letter Grade to GPA Conversion Chart: The Complete Reference

Converting letter grades to GPA points is straightforward once you have the right chart. This page gives you every conversion you need — standard grades, plus/minus grades, percentage equivalents, and the weighted scale for honors and AP courses.

## Standard Unweighted Conversion Chart

This is the chart used by most colleges and universities in the United States.

| Letter Grade | Percentage Range | GPA Points |
|---|---|---|
| A+ | 97–100% | 4.0 |
| A | 93–96% | 4.0 |
| A− | 90–92% | 3.7 |
| B+ | 87–89% | 3.3 |
| B | 83–86% | 3.0 |
| B− | 80–82% | 2.7 |
| C+ | 77–79% | 2.3 |
| C | 73–76% | 2.0 |
| C− | 70–72% | 1.7 |
| D+ | 67–69% | 1.3 |
| D | 63–66% | 1.0 |
| D− | 60–62% | 0.7 |
| F | Below 60% | 0.0 |

Note: Percentage cutoffs vary by institution. Some schools set the A threshold at 90%, others at 94%. Always check your school's specific grading scale.

## How to Convert Your Grades to GPA

1. Find each letter grade in the chart above and note the corresponding GPA points.
2. Multiply the GPA points by the number of credit hours for that course.
3. Add up all the results (called quality points).
4. Divide by total credit hours.

**Quick example:**

| Course | Grade | Points | Credits | Quality Points |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Math | B+ | 3.3 | 3 | 9.9 |
| Biology | A | 4.0 | 4 | 16.0 |
| English | B | 3.0 | 3 | 9.0 |
| History | A− | 3.7 | 3 | 11.1 |

Total quality points: 46.0 — Total credits: 13 — **GPA: 46.0 ÷ 13 = 3.54**

## How Colleges Use Grade Conversions

When colleges evaluate transfer students or international applicants, they convert grades from different systems to the 4.0 scale. Here is what that process looks like:

**Transfer students.** Colleges receive official transcripts with letter grades. Admissions offices apply their own conversion table — which may differ slightly from the chart above — to calculate a transfer GPA. An 89% B+ at your old school counts as 3.3 at most places, but verify with the specific institution.

**Graduate school applications.** Graduate programs almost always request a cumulative GPA on the 4.0 scale. They calculate it from your official transcript, not your school's weighted version. A 4.3 weighted high school GPA becomes a 4.0 in their system.

For more on this topic, see [*Cumulative GPA Calculator Guide: What It Is and How to Calculate It*](/blog/cumulative-gpa-calculator-guide).

**International transcript conversion.** Grades from other countries (percentage marks, letter grades on different scales) are converted by credential evaluation services like WES. They produce a U.S.-equivalent GPA on the 4.0 scale.

## Schools That Use A+ = 4.3

A minority of institutions award 4.3 for an A+, which means a GPA above 4.0 is technically possible on an unweighted scale. Yale, Princeton, and a handful of others use this system. If you are applying to or attending such a school, your chart would look like this for the top end:

| Grade | GPA Points |
|---|---|
| A+ | 4.3 |
| A | 4.0 |
| A− | 3.7 |

All other grades remain the same.

## Grades That Don't Convert

Some grades appear on transcripts but do not enter GPA calculations:

- **P (Pass) / F (Fail)** — Pass earns credit but no quality points. It does not affect GPA.
- **W (Withdrawal)** — Does not affect GPA but appears on the transcript.
- **I (Incomplete)** — Temporarily excluded until a final grade is assigned.
- **AU (Audit)** — No credit, no GPA impact.

## The Bottom Line

The standard 4.0 scale is consistent across most U.S. institutions. An A earns 4.0, a B earns 3.0, and so on down the chart. Plus and minus grades add precision in steps of 0.3–0.7.
