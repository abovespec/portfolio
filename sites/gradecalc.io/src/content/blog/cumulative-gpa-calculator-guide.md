# Cumulative GPA Calculator Guide: What It Is and How to Calculate It

Your cumulative GPA is the single number that summarizes your entire academic record. It follows you to job applications, graduate school forms, honor society eligibility checks, and graduation audits. Understanding how it is calculated — and how to track it yourself — puts you in control of your academic trajectory.

## What Is Cumulative GPA?

Cumulative GPA is the weighted average of every grade you have earned across all semesters at your institution. Every course, every credit hour, every grade contributes — including the bad semesters.

This is different from your semester GPA, which only reflects one term. Cumulative GPA is the long-running total.

## The Formula

```
Cumulative GPA = Total Quality Points ÷ Total Credit Hours Attempted
```

**Quality points** for a course = GPA points × credit hours

For example: A B+ (3.3) in a 4-credit course = 13.2 quality points.

## Step-by-Step Manual Calculation

**Step 1: List every graded course with its credit hours and letter grade.**

**Step 2: Convert each letter grade to GPA points.**

| Grade | GPA Points |
|---|---|
| A / A+ | 4.0 |
| A− | 3.7 |
| B+ | 3.3 |
| B | 3.0 |
| B− | 2.7 |
| C+ | 2.3 |
| C | 2.0 |
| C− | 1.7 |
| D | 1.0 |
| F | 0.0 |

**Step 3: Multiply each course's GPA points by its credit hours to get quality points.**

**Step 4: Sum all quality points. Sum all attempted credit hours.**

**Step 5: Divide total quality points by total credit hours.**

## Worked Example: Two Semesters

### Semester 1

| Course | Grade | GPA Points | Credits | Quality Points |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| English Composition | A | 4.0 | 3 | 12.0 |
| College Algebra | B | 3.0 | 3 | 9.0 |
| Intro to Psychology | A− | 3.7 | 3 | 11.1 |
| Biology I | C+ | 2.3 | 4 | 9.2 |
| **Semester totals** | | | **13** | **41.3** |

Semester 1 GPA: 41.3 ÷ 13 = **3.18**

### Semester 2

| Course | Grade | GPA Points | Credits | Quality Points |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| English Literature | B+ | 3.3 | 3 | 9.9 |
| Statistics | A | 4.0 | 3 | 12.0 |
| Biology II | B | 3.0 | 4 | 12.0 |
| History of Science | A | 4.0 | 3 | 12.0 |
| **Semester totals** | | | **13** | **45.9** |

Semester 2 GPA: 45.9 ÷ 13 = **3.53**

### Cumulative GPA After Two Semesters

- Total quality points: 41.3 + 45.9 = **87.2**
- Total credit hours: 13 + 13 = **26**
- Cumulative GPA: 87.2 ÷ 26 = **3.35**

Notice that cumulative GPA (3.35) falls between the two semester GPAs (3.18 and 3.53). It is always a weighted average of everything — the good and the bad.

## What Counts Toward Cumulative GPA?

| Included | Not Included |
|---|---|
| Graded courses (A–F) | Pass/Fail courses (Pass grade) |
| Repeated courses (both attempts at most schools) | Withdrawn courses (W) |
| Transfer courses (at some schools) | Audited courses |
| Failed courses (F = 0.0) | Incomplete grades (until resolved) |

Always check your institution's academic policies. Some schools exclude transfer credits from cumulative GPA; others include them fully.

## How Cumulative GPA Builds Over Time

Early semesters have outsized influence because there are fewer total credits. A 2.5 in your first semester of 15 credits pulls your GPA down more than a 2.5 in a later semester once you have 90 credits in the bank.

This has a practical implication: the earlier you raise your grades, the easier it is to lift your cumulative GPA. By junior or senior year, each new semester moves the needle less.

**Impact of credit accumulation:**

## Common Mistakes in Manual Calculation

**Forgetting to weight by credit hours.** A 4-credit course contributes four times as many quality points as a 1-credit course. Averaging GPA points directly without weighting gives the wrong answer.

**Excluding failed retakes.** Most schools include the original F and the retake in cumulative GPA calculations. Grade forgiveness or replacement policies are the exception, not the rule.

**Using semester GPA instead of cumulative GPA on applications.** Graduate school forms ask for cumulative GPA. Submitting your best semester GPA instead is both inaccurate and easy for admissions offices to catch.
