> **Disclaimer:** Tipping is voluntary. Amounts reflect common US norms; customs vary internationally.

Hotels involve more tipping touchpoints than almost any other service context. Most travelers tip at checkout but miss the staff who provided the most labor. Here's a complete breakdown by role.

## Housekeeping: The Most Commonly Skipped Tip

Housekeeping is the role most frequently undertipped because the workers are rarely seen and most guests don't think to leave cash. Housekeeping staff at most US hotels earn low hourly wages with variable tip income. The standard guidance:

**$2–5 per night, left each morning.**

Leave it each morning rather than at checkout for two reasons: different staff may clean your room on different days, and checkout day often involves a different crew. Put the cash on the pillow or in a visible spot with a note that says "housekeeping" — this prevents any ambiguity about whether it's intentional.

| Hotel Type | Suggested Daily Tip |
|---|---|
| Budget / economy hotel | $2–3/night |
| Mid-range hotel | $3–5/night |
| Upscale / full-service hotel | $5/night |
| Luxury hotel | $5–10/night |

For a stay with unusual circumstances — you had a large number of extra towels, extra guests, made an unusual mess, or requested extra amenities — tip at the higher end.

## Bellhop / Luggage Assistance

Bellhops carry your bags to your room and often provide a brief orientation to the room and amenities. The standard tip:

**$1–2 per bag, with a $2 minimum per trip.**

A single carry-on bag: $2. Two checked-style bags: $4–5. Multiple large bags or heavy luggage: $5+. If the bellhop also explains the room, sets up something for you, or handles special requests during the same interaction, round up.

## Valet Parking

Valet parking involves two interactions: drop-off and pickup. You do not need to tip at drop-off (though you can). Tip at pickup when you retrieve your car.

**$3–5 per retrieval is the standard tip.** At luxury hotels, $5–10 is more typical. If you retrieve the car multiple times in a day, tip each time.

| Hotel Type | Valet Tip at Pickup |
|---|---|
| Standard hotel | $3–5 |
| Upscale hotel | $5 |
| Luxury hotel | $5–10 |

## Concierge

Tipping the concierge depends on what they do for you. For standard questions (directions, restaurant recommendations, general information), no tip is expected — this is part of their job. For real effort:

| Service | Suggested Tip |
|---|---|
| Simple restaurant recommendation | No tip needed |
| Made a reservation for you | $5–10 |
| Secured a difficult reservation | $10–20 |
| Arranged special access, tickets, or events | $15–25+ |
| Resolved a significant problem on your behalf | $15–20+ |

Tip the concierge when you receive the service, not at checkout. Cash is easiest.

## Room Service

Room service almost always includes a service charge and sometimes a delivery fee on the bill. Check your receipt. If a service charge is already added (typically 15–20%), you're not obligated to add more — though a $1–2 cash tip directly to the person who brought the tray is still appreciated since service charges may or may not reach the delivery staff.

If no service charge appears on the bill, treat it like restaurant service: **15–20% tip.**

## Door Staff

The doorman who hails a taxi for you warrants a $1–2 tip. If a doorman handles luggage, treats it like a bellhop tip ($1–2 per bag). Getting you a taxi or rideshare in difficult weather or heavy traffic: $2–3.

## Pool and Beach Attendants

At resort properties with pool or beach attendants who set up chairs, bring towels, or provide drink service:

- Setting up chairs and delivering towels: $2–3 per day
- Bringing drinks or snacks tableside: $1–2 per item (or treat like bar service)

## Checkout Tip for Extended Stays

At the end of a multi-night stay, it's reasonable to leave a final housekeeping tip at checkout for the staff who cleaned on your last day, in addition to daily tips. This isn't required if you've been tipping daily.

## Practical Tips for Tipping at Hotels

- **Carry small bills.** Most hotel tipping requires $1s, $5s, and $10s. ATM withdrawals rarely produce these denominations — get them before you arrive or request them at the front desk.
- **Tip at the time of service.** Trying to find staff at checkout is unreliable.
- **"Gratuity included" on a bill** means you don't need to add to it, though a small cash tip to the individual is still welcomed.

## Quick Reference

| Role | When | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Housekeeping | Each morning | $2–5/night |
| Bellhop | At room arrival | $1–2/bag, $2 min |
| Valet | Car retrieval | $3–5 ($5–10 luxury) |
| Concierge — reservation | When service rendered | $5–10 |
| Concierge — special access | When service rendered | $15–25 |
| Room service (no service charge) | At delivery | 15–20% |
| Doorman (taxi hail) | After taxi arrives | $1–2 |
