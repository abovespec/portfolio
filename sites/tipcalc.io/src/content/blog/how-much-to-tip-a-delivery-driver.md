> **Disclaimer:** Tipping amounts reflect commonly cited US norms and are voluntary gratuities, not mandatory fees. Platform pay structures change frequently; verify current terms on each platform.

Delivery drivers are independent contractors on most major platforms, which means their pay structures differ significantly from restaurant servers — and the tip math changes accordingly.

## What the Platforms Actually Pay

Understanding driver pay explains why tips matter more for some orders than others.

| Platform | Base Pay Structure | Tip Handling |
|---|---|---|
| DoorDash | Per-order base ($2–$10+, varies by distance/time) | 100% goes to driver |
| Uber Eats | Per-order + per-mile rate | 100% goes to driver |
| Grubhub | Per-order + per-mile rate | 100% goes to driver |
| Instacart | Per-batch base (often $7–$10 for a full shop) | 100% goes to driver |
| Amazon Flex | Hourly rate ($18–$25/hr depending on market) | Tips on top |

DoorDash, Uber Eats, and Grubhub removed the practice of applying tips to base pay guarantees, meaning your tip now goes directly to the driver. That wasn't always true — in earlier years, some platforms used customer tips to subsidize their own guaranteed minimums.

## Standard Tipping Guidance

**For food delivery (DoorDash, Uber Eats, Grubhub):**

The commonly cited minimum is **$3–5 for a standard order under 5 miles in normal conditions**. A percentage-based approach (15–20% of the order total) often works better for larger orders and naturally scales with your bill.

| Order Size | Minimum Floor | Percentage Equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| Under $20 | $3–4 | — (use flat amount) |
| $20–$40 | $4–6 | 15–20% |
| $40–$60 | $6–10 | 15–20% |
| $60+ | $10+ | 15–20% |

The flat minimum matters because a 15% tip on a $15 order is only $2.25 — not enough to cover a driver who traveled several miles and waited at a restaurant. When the percentage produces less than $3, use the flat floor instead.

**For grocery delivery (Instacart, Shipt):**

Grocery shoppers invest more time per order — they're navigating a full store, selecting produce, handling substitutions, and loading heavy bags. The Instacart default tip is 5%, but that undersells the work. **10–20% is a more appropriate range**, with a $5 minimum on small orders.

For large grocery hauls ($150+), 10% lands in the right range. For a typical household weekly shop ($80–$120), $10–15 is reasonable.

## When to Tip More

Certain conditions justify going above the baseline:

- **Bad weather** — rain, snow, and extreme heat make the job genuinely harder and riskier. Add $2–4 above your standard.
- **Long distances** — apps show estimated mileage. A driver traveling 8–10 miles deserves more than one going 2 miles.
- **Large or heavy orders** — cases of water, multiple large bags, and awkward items require real physical effort.
- **Apartment buildings and stairs** — especially without elevator access, this is extra work.
- **Late night or early morning** — orders placed outside peak hours mean less driver density and sometimes longer waits.
- **Holiday and weekend orders** — drivers who work holidays are giving up personal time.

## Pre-Tip vs. Adjusting After Delivery

Most apps require a tip at checkout, before delivery. You can adjust the tip after the fact on DoorDash (within 30 days) and Uber Eats (within a short window after delivery). If service was clearly exceptional — or if something went wrong that was the driver's fault — it's reasonable to adjust after the fact.

Reducing a tip punitively for restaurant errors (wrong items, slow kitchen) isn't fair to the driver, who had no control over the order contents. If a restaurant packed your bag wrong, that's not a tipping offense against the driver.

## Should You Tip Through the App or Cash?

Both methods work. Tipping in-app is tracked by the platform and counts toward a driver's metrics on some platforms. Cash tips are immediate and unambiguous. Either is fine — the driver gets the money either way.

## Quick Reference

| Scenario | Suggested Tip |
|---|---|
| Standard food delivery, good conditions | 15–20% or $4–6 minimum |
| Food delivery in rain/snow | Add $2–4 above standard |
| Small grocery order (under $50) | $5–8 |
| Full grocery shop ($80–$150) | $10–15 |
| Very large grocery order ($150+) | 10% ($15+) |
| Long distance (8+ miles) | Add $2–3 above standard |

## The Bottom Line

Delivery drivers are independent contractors absorbing vehicle costs, gas, and wear without employer benefits. The apps don't cover these costs — tip income is a meaningful part of whether a driver's shift is worth taking. **$3–5 is a floor, not a target.** For most food orders, 15–20% gets to the right place; for grocery delivery, 10–20% reflects the extra labor involved.
