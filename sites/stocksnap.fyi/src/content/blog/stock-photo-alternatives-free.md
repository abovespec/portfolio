Most people discover one free stock photo site, get comfortable with it, and never look further. That works until it doesn't — until you need a photo your go-to site doesn't have, or until you notice the same images appearing on competitor sites, or until licensing ambiguity causes a problem.

There are eight sites worth knowing. Each has a distinct character, a different library focus, and a different set of trade-offs. Here's an honest assessment of each.

## 1. Unsplash

**What it is:** The default choice for millions of creators. Unsplash has an enormous library of contributor-uploaded photography, and the quality ceiling is genuinely high.

**License:** Custom Unsplash License — no attribution required, free for commercial use. Not CC0, but functionally similar. Restrictions: cannot resell or redistribute Unsplash photos as stock photos themselves.

**Pros:**
- Massive library (millions of photos)
- High quality photography — real professionals contribute
- Good search with decent category filtering
- No signup required for basic downloads
- Integration with Figma, Sketch, and other design tools

**Cons:**
- Popular photos appear on hundreds of sites — originality suffers
- AI-generated content is mixed in without clear labeling on some uploads
- No video content
- Search is keyword-only; no visual similarity search

**Best for:** General-purpose photography, lifestyle content, editorial imagery. Use when you need a photo of something real and specific.

## 2. Pexels

**What it is:** Similar to Unsplash in scope, but with video content alongside photos. Pexels has grown substantially and now rivals Unsplash in both size and quality.

**License:** Pexels License — no attribution required, free for commercial use. Similar restrictions to Unsplash.

**Pros:**
- Both photos and videos in one place
- Quality has improved significantly — less filler than a few years ago
- Good API for developers who need programmatic access
- "Popular" and "Latest" sort options are genuinely useful for finding fresh content

**Cons:**
- Some model releases are ambiguous for commercial use
- Interface can feel cluttered
- Less editorial curation than Unsplash at the top end

**Best for:** Content creators who need both photo and video assets. Also good if you want an alternative search to cross-reference against Unsplash results.

## 3. Pixabay

**What it is:** The oldest of the major free stock sites, with a more eclectic mix of content — photography alongside vector art, illustrations, and clip art.

**License:** Pixabay Content License — no attribution required, free for commercial use.

**Pros:**
- Widest content variety — photos, vectors, illustrations, GIFs, video, music
- Good for finding things you can't get elsewhere (icons, clip art, abstract graphics)
- Clear commercial use labeling per image

**Cons:**
- Quality is inconsistent — useful content is mixed with low-quality uploads
- Interface is older and less polished
- Search requires more effort to surface good results

**Best for:** When you need something specific that doesn't exist as photography — an icon, an illustration, a diagram-like graphic. Also good for free music and sound effects.

## 4. Burst by Shopify

**What it is:** A stock photo library built by Shopify for entrepreneurs. Every photo is selected with commercial use in mind — product shots, lifestyle imagery, small business contexts.

**License:** Standard CC0 or custom Burst License, depending on the photo. Both allow commercial use without attribution.

**Pros:**
- Strongly practical focus — real products, real people, real business contexts
- Model releases are explicitly noted
- Curated for e-commerce specifically — great for product category photography

**Cons:**
- Much smaller library than Unsplash or Pexels
- Niche focus means it's less useful for general content creation
- Not updated as frequently as the larger platforms

**Best for:** E-commerce, small business marketing, product category imagery.

## 5. StockSnap.fyi

**What it is:** An AI-generated stock photo library focused on seven curated niches: abstract backgrounds, bokeh, food flatlay, minimalist workspace, interiors, nature macro, and sci-tech.

**License:** Free for commercial use, no attribution required, no signup needed.

**Pros:**
- Fully AI-generated — no model releases, no property releases, no attribution complications
- 980 curated images across seven visual categories
- Consistent quality within each niche
- No account required — download immediately
- WebP format for better web performance
- Not circulating on other stock sites — less risk of seeing your images elsewhere

**Cons:**
- Smaller library than traditional stock sites
- Limited to seven niches — not suitable for general photography needs
- No real people (by design, given AI generation)

**Best for:** Background images, header images, presentation backgrounds, social media imagery. The AI-native option for abstract, atmospheric, and stylized content.

## 6. Reshot

**What it is:** A curated free stock photo site that focuses on authenticity — real scenes, genuine emotion, less staged-looking content.

**License:** Reshot License — free for commercial use, no attribution required.

**Pros:**
- More authentic, less stock-looking photography
- Niche collections organized around concepts (entrepreneurship, wellness, community)
- Smaller library means less filler

**Cons:**
- Smaller library overall
- Search is less powerful than Unsplash or Pexels
- Not frequently updated

**Best for:** Brands that want to avoid the polished, staged look of traditional stock — particularly useful for human-centered lifestyle content.

## 7. Gratisography

**What it is:** A quirky, distinctly stylized free stock photo site from photographer Ryan McGuire. The photos are intentionally unusual — often humorous, surreal, or whimsical.

**License:** CC0 — completely free, no attribution, no restrictions.

**Pros:**
- Genuinely unique imagery you won't find anywhere else
- CC0 — cleaner license than most
- Strong visual identity that makes content stand out

**Cons:**
- Very small library
- Specific aesthetic that doesn't work for all content types
- Not suitable for professional or corporate contexts

**Best for:** Creative agencies, quirky brands, content that benefits from unexpected visuals.

## 8. Kaboompics

**What it is:** A free stock photo library run by Polish photographer Karolina Grabowska. Known for its consistent aesthetic: warm tones, natural light, lifestyle and interior photography.

**License:** Kaboompics License — free for commercial and non-commercial use, no attribution required.

**Pros:**
- Very strong aesthetic consistency — warm, European lifestyle feel
- Excellent for interior, food, and workspace photography
- Palette data provided for each photo (useful for designers matching brand colors)
- No signup required

**Cons:**
- One photographer's perspective — limited in variety
- Strong style means it's not neutral enough for all contexts
- Less suitable for corporate or tech content

**Best for:** Lifestyle, food, interior, and workspace photography with a warm, natural aesthetic.

## Which Site Should You Use?

There's no single answer — the right choice depends on what you're looking for:

| Need | Best option |
|------|-------------|
| General photography, large library | Unsplash |
| Photo + video in one place | Pexels |
| Vectors, illustrations, mixed media | Pixabay |
| E-commerce and product contexts | Burst |
| Abstract backgrounds, bokeh, AI-generated | StockSnap.fyi |
| Authentic, unstaged people photography | Reshot |
| Quirky, unusual visual content | Gratisography |
| Warm lifestyle and interior photography | Kaboompics |

The practical move is to bookmark three or four and search them in parallel when you need something. Your primary site will cover most needs. Your secondary and tertiary picks will cover the gaps. And when you need something in a category no photographer has ever bothered to shoot — an abstract tech background at 3am, a perfectly minimalist workspace with no specific brands — an AI library like StockSnap.fyi covers territory that traditional photography doesn't.
