## What "No Attribution" Actually Means

Attribution is a legal requirement tied to copyright. When a photographer releases an image under certain licenses, they retain the right to be credited. Skipping the credit isn't just impolite — it's technically a license violation.

The most common licenses you'll encounter:

**CC0 (Creative Commons Zero)** — The photographer waives all rights, including the right to attribution. You can use the photo for anything, including commercial projects, without crediting anyone. This is the gold standard for "no strings attached."

**CC BY (Creative Commons Attribution)** — Free to use, but you must credit the creator. This is what most people think of when they hear "free photos" — but it's not truly attribution-free.

**Custom royalty-free licenses** — Many stock photo sites use their own licenses that allow commercial use without payment, but may still require a credit line or prohibit certain use cases (like using a person's likeness in political ads).

The word "royalty-free" does not mean "attribution-free." Royalty-free just means you don't pay per-use. You can still owe attribution.

## Why Most "Free" Sites Still Technically Require Attribution

Unsplash, Pexels, and similar sites are generous to photographers — they let anyone upload photos and set the terms. The platforms encourage photographers to release under permissive licenses, but they also let photographers opt into attribution requirements or usage restrictions.

The result: most photos on these platforms are effectively attribution-free in practice (almost nobody enforces it), but the license may technically require credit. If you're building a commercial product or publishing professionally, "almost nobody enforces it" isn't good enough.

For truly clean licensing, you need photos that are explicitly CC0, or from a platform that legally guarantees no attribution is needed.

## Sites That Genuinely Don't Require Attribution

### Unsplash

Unsplash uses its own license (not CC0), which explicitly states: "You do not have to credit the creator or Unsplash." This is legally clear. The library is enormous — millions of professional-quality photos — and no credit is required.

The catch: some Unsplash photos have model or property releases that restrict commercial use. Check individual photo pages if you're using images commercially.

### Pexels

Pexels uses a similar custom license: no attribution required, free for commercial use. The library has millions of photos and videos. Quality varies widely — there's stunning professional photography alongside mediocre mobile uploads.

Pexels also hosts video, which Unsplash doesn't. If you need motion content, Pexels is worth bookmarking.

### Pixabay

Pixabay operates under its own Content License that permits commercial use with no attribution required. It predates CC0 but functions similarly. Pixabay's library is eclectic — you'll find everything from high-quality photography to clip art and illustrations.

One useful feature: Pixabay clearly labels whether media can be used commercially, so filtering is easy.

### Burst by Shopify

Burst was built specifically for entrepreneurs who need product and lifestyle photos for their online stores. The library is smaller than Unsplash or Pexels, but every photo is practical — real products, real people, real settings. No attribution required, and photos are specifically chosen for commercial use cases.

## How to Verify a License Before You Download

Even when a site claims "no attribution required," it's worth taking thirty seconds to confirm:

1. **Read the license page directly** — don't rely on summaries. Look for phrases like "no attribution required" or "attribution not required." If the license says "attribution optional" or "appreciated but not required," that's still technically optional.

2. **Check if the photo has additional model or property releases** — especially important for images with recognizable people or branded products.

3. **Note any commercial use restrictions** — some licenses permit editorial use (journalism, blog posts) but not commercial use (ads, products for sale).

4. **Save a record** — if you're using a photo commercially, keep a screenshot of the license page at the time you downloaded. License terms can change.

## Practical Tips for Using Attribution-Free Photos

**Resize before uploading.** Most stock sites serve large originals — sometimes 6,000+ pixels wide. Resize to your actual display size. This reduces page weight and improves load time without affecting visual quality.

**Use descriptive alt text.** Even if the photo needs no attribution, it still needs good alt text for accessibility and SEO. Describe what's actually in the image.

**Don't redistribute or resell.** Even CC0 images can't be packaged and resold as stock photos themselves on most platforms. "No attribution" doesn't mean you can create a competing stock photo site from the images.

**AI-generated photos are naturally release-free.** If you need photos of people for commercial use, AI-generated images avoid the model release problem entirely. No real person appears in the photo, so there's no release to worry about.
